### 0:30–3:00 · 1. The clerk gap after closing (slide 2)
Every one of us assumes the hard part of a bond deal is the legal part. Then the deal closes and the promises go into a binder nobody opens until something breaks.

Three failures I have watched. An annual report goes late because the one person who knew the deadline left in March. A rent roll goes out on time, to the wrong address, and nobody can prove it went. A rebate computation is due in year five, and year five is not on a calendar because there is no calendar.

None of those needed a lawyer to prevent. They needed a clerk who never forgets and never guesses. That is the gap. That is all we are here to close.

### 3:00–6:00 · The line (slide 3)
Before anything else, the line. [Read the control sentence on screen.] That is in our engagement letter, on our website, and in our code. We record and transcribe what your professionals approve. We do not decide which clause controls, we do not interpret a deadline formula, we do not decide whether an obligation applies.

Why lead with it? Because it is the reason you can hand the file to your counsel and your board. A register that quietly contains our opinions is worth less than no register. A register that contains only what your professionals approved is something you can defend.

One more honest phrase: this method is activity-reviewed, not counsel-cleared. We say that out loud.

### 6:00–9:00 · 2. What a working book contains (slide 4)
A working book has six things and nothing else. The register: every undertaking bound to the document and the clause that created it. The calendar: only dates your professionals approved in writing. The evidence vault index: every file you delivered, named by obligation and period. The gap list: an approved undertaking with no file for it, and it stops there. Items awaiting input: what we asked your counsel for and have not received. And the refusal log: every judgment question anyone asked us, the date, and where it went.

Notice what is not on the list. No score. No plan. No opinion. What you will see on the sample in a few minutes is exactly what a client gets.

### 9:00–11:00 · 3. Transcribe, compare, refuse (slide 5)
Three verbs. Transcribe: read every clause and quote the duty and the deadline language word for word. We never paraphrase a deadline, because a paraphrase is an interpretation. Compare: put each approved row beside the files in the vault. Is there a file, does it match the period. That is the entire test. Refuse: is it material, are we late, which clause controls. Logged, routed, left unanswered.

There is no model deciding anything in this pipeline. The transcription and the comparison are mechanical, which means they can be tested and replayed and explained to a board.

### 11:00–14:00 · 4. Four status codes, no fifth (slide 6)
Every row carries one of four words. Filed: a matching file is in the vault. Not filed: the date is in the future. Evidence missing: the date passed and there is no matching file in what you gave us. Not testable: there is no calendar date and no event was supplied.

People always ask for a fifth: "needs a professional." It feels careful. It is the opposite. The moment you mark some rows that way, the rows you did not mark read as cleared. Nobody has to write the word "compliant" for a reader to infer it. So there is no fifth. The routing lives in two lists instead, requests and refusals, and neither says anything about the obligation.

### 24:00–27:00 · Your question (slide 10)
[Take two or three live questions. Sort each one out loud before answering.]

**Custody answer pattern.** "In the book that would be a row under the loan agreement, section such-and-such, recipient the trustee, frequency quarterly. The evidence that would count is the report itself with the delivery date. The row would be named OB-number, period, short name."

**Judgment answer pattern.** "That is a question of whether it applies, and that belongs with your bond counsel or dissemination agent. What the book would do is record the clause, log your question with today's date, and leave the field empty until they answer in writing. I would rather tell you that plainly than guess."

Both answers are useful. The second one is the product.
