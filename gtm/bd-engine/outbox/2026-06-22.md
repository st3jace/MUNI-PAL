## Content (1 post) — Angle 3: Hidden-Cost Spotlight

**Topic: "The back-and-forth tax."** (Honest positioning — describes our system, cites no client numbers.)

> Ask an operator what the expensive part of a conduit bond deal is and they'll say the interest rate. Watch one actually happen and it's something else entirely: **the back-and-forth tax.**
>
> It's the request that goes operator → counsel → advisor → operator, four times, because the first answer was in the wrong format. It's the document you "already sent" that gets asked for again because no one owns the master list. It's the two-week gap between "we need your last three years of financials" and "here they are, reconciled the way underwriters expect."
>
> None of it shows up on the term sheet. All of it shows up on the calendar — and the calendar is where deals stall and sponsors lose faith.
>
> The fix isn't working harder during the deal. It's having the package **assembled and in underwriter-ready format before the process starts**, with one owner of the request list. That's most of what "bond-ready" actually means — and it's the part nobody quotes you a price for.
>
> We built the system we use to do exactly that on live engagements. If you've got a facility project and the "you're not ready yet" you keep hearing has never come with a checklist, that's the conversation I give away free.
>
> What's the worst document-request loop you've lived through? 👇
