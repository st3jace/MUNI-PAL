**Angle 3 · Hidden-cost spotlight · fresh cut: "readiness has a shelf life."** *(Distinct from prior angle-3 cuts: 06-22 document-churn, 06-29 board-calendar-slip, 07-06 premature-no, 07-13 carry-clock. This one prices re-buying diligence, not idle capacity. Honest positioning — no client numbers, proof library empty.)*

- [ ] **Post draft:**
  > Everyone models the cost of a stalled bond deal as *waiting.* The bigger, quieter cost is *expiration.*
  >
  > Readiness isn't a document — it's a set of perishables:
  > • Audited financials go stale and need a refresh.
  > • Appraisals typically carry a 6–12 month shelf life.
  > • Market/feasibility studies age out of "current."
  > • Credit approvals and rate assumptions lapse.
  >
  > So a deal that slips two quarters doesn't just arrive late. It arrives having to *re-buy work you already paid for* — the same appraisal, the same study, another trip through committee. The delay tax isn't the wait; it's the re-underwrite.
  >
  > The operators who avoid it treat their readiness package as a *living file* with owners and refresh dates, not a binder assembled once for one closing. That's the difference between "we're ready" being a status and being a snapshot that already expired.
  >
  > If your project might go to a conduit bond in the next 18 months: which of those four perishables is already past its date?
