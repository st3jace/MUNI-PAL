# Adventist Baseline Interview — Script

**Format:** 45 minutes, video call, two Launch Shop people present (CEO + Bond Strategist). One leads, one takes notes. Recorded with permission.
**Audience:** Adventist CFO or VP Finance + ideally one direct staffer who actually executed the last deal day-to-day.
**Purpose:** Capture baseline data for the pilot **and** discover what they actually want from a service like Muni-Pal. Half measurement, half discovery. Do not let it become a sales call.

> **Tone note:** This is not a pitch and not a vendor intake form. It is a peer conversation between two organizations that have already decided to work together on a pilot. The goal is to leave with a clear baseline document AND a clear sense of whether they would tell a peer system about us.

## Part 1 — Framing (5 min)

**Lead:** "Thanks for the time. I want to be upfront about what this conversation is and isn't. We're going to do two things: first, get a baseline on how your last comparable deal actually ran — hours, milestones, where the friction was — so we have something honest to compare against after this pilot closes. Second, I want to understand what would actually make a service like ours worth your time and money, beyond the pilot. I'm going to ask some pointed questions in the second half. The honest answers help us more than the polite ones."

"The whole call is about 45 minutes. We're recording for our notes, with your permission — is that OK?"

[Get explicit consent on recording. If no, take detailed notes and circulate within 24 hours for correction.]

---

## Part 2 — Baseline: Their Last Comparable Deal (15 min)

Anchor on the specific prior deal you identified. Reference it by par, year, and use of proceeds. This signals you did your homework and saves 10 minutes of context-setting.

### Timeline reconstruction
- "When did you formally engage your underwriter and counsel for that deal? Roughly — month is fine."
- "When did you price?"
- "Walk me through the milestones in between as you remember them. POS, rating agency presentations, roadshow, pricing — what stretched and what moved fast?"
- "Was there any point where the deal *waited* on something — a document, a sign-off, a piece of analysis — for more than a week?"

Capture: kickoff date, pricing date, milestone-level dates (best effort), explicit "wait points."

### Hours reconstruction
- "Roughly how much of your own time did that deal consume? I'm going to give you buckets so you don't have to be precise: under 10 hours, 10–25, 25–50, 50–100, 100–250, more than 250. Across the whole deal, kickoff to close."
- "And your direct staff — who was involved, and roughly which buckets for them?"
- "Outside counsel, MA, UW counsel — do you have a sense of their hours, even just from their bills?"

Capture: each role, bucket. Note: do NOT push for precision. Buckets are deliberately wide because false precision is worse than honest ranges.

### Deal team composition (critical for legal path)
- "Who was your municipal advisor on that deal? Are they your MA on this upcoming deal as well?"
- "If the MA changed between deals, who is it now, and why the change?"
- "Who was lead underwriter? Same on this deal?"

**Why this matters:** Our legal path (the §15B(e)(4)(C) exclusion or IRMA-adjacent analysis) depends on Adventist having a registered MA on this deal. If they don't, the engagement structure has to change before anything is signed. If the MA changed between deals, the baseline hours/timeline comparison is confounded — different MAs have different work styles.

### COI perception
- "Do you remember what your total cost of issuance was on that deal?"
- "Did that number surprise you — high, low, about what you expected?"
- "Did you compare it to anything? Other issuers, prior deals, a rule of thumb?"

**Why this matters:** If they never really looked at their COI, COI benchmarking is not a pain point for this borrower and we should not lead with it. If they have strong opinions about their COI, we have a wedge.

### Friction inventory (this is the gold)
- "If you could re-do that deal, what would you change about how it ran? Not the terms — the *process*."
- "What was the most annoying part? Not the hardest, the *most annoying*."
- "Was there a piece of work that you knew was busywork while you were doing it?"
- "Did anything blow up at the eleventh hour that, in hindsight, you wish someone had caught earlier?"

Capture verbatim quotes where possible. These quotes become the language for both the post-mortem report and any future marketing copy. People do not buy "31% displacement" — they buy "I never want to fight with rating agencies about that schedule again."

## Part 3 — Discovery: Demand and Appetite (20 min)

**This is the part the pilot is really about.** The productivity numbers we collect during the deal are nice. The answers to these questions tell us whether there's a business.

### Willingness — direct
- "Suppose this pilot goes well. What would 'going well' look like to you? In your own words, not ours."
- "If we did this for your next deal — not a pilot, a real engagement — what would you expect to pay for it? I'm not negotiating, I'm trying to understand how you'd think about pricing it."
- "What's the unit you'd price it in? Per deal? Per million of par? Subscription? Success fee tied to COI savings?"

**If they say "I have no idea" — deploy the anchor:**
> "For context, a municipal advisor on a deal this size would typically charge in the range of $1.50–$2.50 per $1,000 of par — so on a $200M deal, roughly $300K to $500K. We're not an MA and we're not replacing one. We sit alongside your MA and handle the readiness and coordination work. Does that frame help you think about what this would be worth?"

Listen for: where they land after the anchor. "Fraction of an MA fee" is the right frame. If they push back on the anchor ("our MA doesn't charge that much" / "that seems high"), write down the number they quote — that's real market intel on Adventist's MA pricing.

**Pre-call internal anchor (do NOT share with Adventist):** Launch Shop's tiers are Readiness $15K–25K, Standard $40K–50K, Accelerator $75K+. Use these to interpret what you hear, not as an ask.

Listen for: do they have a frame for pricing this at all? Even after the anchor, if they still say "I have no idea" — that itself is data. It means the category is novel enough that we'll be teaching the market.

### Willingness — indirect
- "If I described this service to your CFO peer at another health system, in one sentence — what would that sentence have to say for them to take a meeting?"
- "Who else in your organization would have to be comfortable with this for it to become a standing relationship? Treasury? Legal? Board?"
- "Is there anyone who would *block* it? What would their objection be?"

Listen for: org-chart obstacles. A service that the CFO loves but Treasury blocks is a service that doesn't sell.

### Category questions
- "Are you aware of anything else like this on the market? Anything AI-driven, anything that promises to make bond issuance faster or cheaper or less painful?"
- "If you went looking for something like this six months ago, where would you have looked? Who would you have asked?"
- "When the next deal comes up, what's the first call you make? Walk me through the decision."

Listen for: distribution. Even a perfect product dies if it's not in the path of how buyers actually decide. We need to know the path.

### The hardest question (ask it last, ask it directly)
- "Honest answer: if this pilot didn't exist and I called you cold to pitch this service, would you have taken the meeting?"

The answer to this question is the most valuable thing in the entire interview. Write it down verbatim.

## Part 4 — Logistics (5 min)

- "For the pilot, we need a few things from you: a weekly 5-minute time diary from you and your staff, a way for us to log the tasks our agents are doing on the deal, and a 30-minute post-mortem after close. Is that workable?"
- "Who's the right day-to-day point of contact on your side? Not you — your champion."
- "Is there anything we should know about how your organization handles vendor relationships, NDAs, security review, anything that could slow us down before we even start?"
- "Specifically on the technology vendor side — do you have a procurement process or IT security review? SOC 2 requirement? BAA? Typical turnaround on vendor onboarding?"

**Why this matters:** Large health systems have 3–6 month vendor onboarding cycles. If Adventist's procurement requires SOC 2 Type II attestation or formal security review, the pilot timeline could slip dramatically. Better to know this now than after the engagement letter is signed.

## After the call

- [ ] Transcribe the recording (or clean up notes) within 24 hours.
- [ ] Fill out `pilot/adventist-2026/baseline.md` with: timeline reconstruction, hours buckets, friction inventory (verbatim quotes), willingness signals, category awareness, distribution path, the answer to the hardest question.
- [ ] Send a thank-you email within 24 hours that summarizes what you heard. Two purposes: confirms the data, and gives them a chance to correct mischaracterizations before the pilot starts.
- [ ] Bond Strategist and CEO debrief separately for 15 minutes. Independent reads of the same conversation surface biases.

---

## What success looks like for this interview

You leave the call with:
1. Defensible baseline numbers (timeline, hours buckets) for §3 of the protocol — even if rough.
2. Three to five verbatim friction quotes you could put on a slide.
3. A clear answer to "would they have taken a cold call." Yes / no / "depends on who called."
4. A pricing frame — even if it's "I have no idea."
5. The name of one peer they'd introduce you to if the pilot works.

If you leave without #3 and #5, you ran a polite call, not a discovery call. Schedule a follow-up.
