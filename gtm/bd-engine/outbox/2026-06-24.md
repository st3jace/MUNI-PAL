## Content (1 post) — Angle 1: Deal Anatomy

**Topic: "What a conduit bond deal actually looks like from the operator's chair — walked stage by stage."** (Honest positioning — anatomy of a *publicly-filed* deal type; no client numbers, no named private engagement.)

> People picture a bond deal as one big event: you "get approved" and the money shows up. From the operator's chair it's nothing like that. Here's the real shape of a conduit / IRB deal, stage by stage — drawn from what's visible in public filings, not any one client:
>
> **Stage 1 — Eligibility.** Before anything, someone has to confirm the project even fits a conduit structure (a manufacturing plant, a recycling facility, affordable housing — wider than most operators assume). This is a one-meeting answer that operators routinely spend months guessing at.
>
> **Stage 2 — The readiness story.** The single biggest time sink, and the one nobody warns you about. Lenders and underwriters don't fund a project; they fund a *narrative backed by paper*. Assembling that the first time, with no template, is where deals stall for quarters.
>
> **Stage 3 — Disclosure package.** The document set that has to survive scrutiny — financials, project specs, the official statement. Built well once, it's reusable for the next deal. Built ad hoc, every deal restarts from zero.
>
> **Stage 4 — Issuer + market.** The conduit issuer (an IDA, a finance authority) lends its tax-exempt status; counsel and the MA get the structure to market. By here the operator's hardest work is already done — if Stage 2 and 3 were done right.
>
> The lesson operators tell me after their first deal: the bottleneck was never the bond. It was getting *deal-ready* with no map. We built the system we use to compress Stages 1–3 on live engagements — the part that turns "two years out" into "a few months of organized work."
>
> Which stage is your project actually stuck at? 👇
