**Angle 5 · Checklist excerpt · FRESH cut — the five items you already have and still fail on: the readiness gap is usually a FORM problem, not a substance problem.** *(Longest-unused usable angle; last ran 07-28. Structurally distinct from every prior angle-5 cut: 06-25 the lender-check, 07-02 the questions a lender will ask, 07-09 the pre-check, 07-16 who owns each line, 07-28 prerequisite-independence — which item needs a project and which doesn't. - [ ] **Post — draft:**

> When an operator gets told they're "not bond-ready," the mental picture is usually of a missing thing — a document that doesn't exist, a milestone not reached. That's occasionally true. More often the item exists, and it's in a form nobody downstream can rely on.

> Five that show up over and over:

> **1. Financial statements.** You have them. What diligence needs is a comparable multi-year presentation across the same entity set, on a consistent basis, that reads as a trend. Three good years prepared three different ways is not three years of history — it's three documents.

> **2. Site control.** You control the site. The question is what the paper says: an LOI and a good relationship are not an option, a lease, or a deed — and whatever it is has to have a term that outlives the financing calendar, not just the current quarter.

> **3. Board or member approval.** The board approved. Do the minutes record the specific authority granted — who can sign what, up to what amount, for how long? "The board is supportive" is a fact about the room. Diligence needs a fact about the record.

> **4. Ownership and structure.** You know exactly who owns what. There's often no single page that shows it: the borrowing entity, what sits above it, who controls, and where the project and the property actually live. That page gets built eventually. Building it early tends to surface a surprise or two while surprises are still cheap.

> **5. Permits and insurance.** They exist. Nobody has laid their expiration and renewal dates against the financing calendar. A permit that lapses in month five of a nine-month process isn't a document problem when it happens — it's a schedule problem.

> The reason this matters: substance gaps take quarters to close. Form gaps take afternoons. Operators who assume they're years away are often looking at a stack of afternoons, and the only way to know which one you're in is to check.

> We built the system we run on our own live engagements to sort exactly this — what's genuinely missing versus what just isn't in a form that will hold. Happy to walk anyone through where they stand, free, whether or not it goes anywhere.
