The Obligation Book Is the Product

How Launch Shop built the 10-Day Obligation Register in one working day on synthetic data, and what that way of working means for everyone who lives with a closed bond deal.

Muni-Pal · a Launch Shop productWritten 2026-09-10Reading time about 25 minutes

A private borrower closes a bond deal and walks away with a binder. Inside it are promises that outlive everyone who negotiated them: annual reports, event notices, certificates, calculations, records. Different deals carry different promises. Nobody owns the calendar. This document explains what we built to fix that, how we built it without crossing the line into advice, and why the same way of working matters to counsel, trustees, issuers, and investors as much as to the borrower.

It has three parts. Part I is the build, in enough technical detail that an engineer could reproduce it. Part II is the argument. Part III is a 30-minute talk that carries both.

Part I · Technical note

How we built the Obligation Register

1. The problem we chose, and the line we would not cross

Post-issuance work has two halves. One half is judgment: which clause controls, whether an event is material, what a deadline formula means for this fiscal year. That half belongs to bond counsel, the municipal advisor, and the dissemination agent, and in the United States it sits behind the municipal advisor definition and Rule 15c2-12. The other half is clerical: find every undertaking, bind it to the clause that created it, keep a calendar, keep the evidence, notice when a file is missing. That half is where the pain is, and almost nobody owns it.

We chose the clerical half. Not by arguing about the boundary, but by changing the artifact until the boundary had nothing to attach to. Pre-issuance work runs into the advisor definition because it is "with respect to the issuance." A register built after closing, from documents the client already has, with every judgment routed to their own professionals, is not.

The move that made it sellable

The winning move was not to argue the gate. It was to change the artifact until the gate had nothing to attach to. The bonds are sold, so "with respect to the issuance" has nothing to bind.

2. Five constraints that shaped every line of code

  1. The approved-input rule. Every row in the register carries written input from the client's counsel, municipal advisor, or dissemination agent. We transcribe. We do not decide which undertaking controls, interpret an ambiguous deadline formula, determine successor or refunding effects, or decide whether an obligation applies.
  2. Four status codes, no fifth. filednot filedevidence missingnot testable A proposed fifth code, "professional determination required," was rejected. Marking a row that way is itself a determination, and the unmarked rows then read as cleared.
  3. Two lists that are not statuses. Items awaiting input, which records what we asked for in writing and have not received. And the refusal log, which records every judgment question put to us, the date, and where it was routed. Neither says anything about the obligation.
  4. No computed dates. Dates enter the calendar only as approved by a professional. The runner never turns "180 days after fiscal year end" into June 29.
  5. No EMMA. Nothing in the build, the demo, the prospect list, or the copy derives from the MSRB's public filing system. Synthetic data and issuer-published records carry the whole method.

3. Architecture

Figure 1. The whole product is one pipeline. Three inputs, one runner, six outputs, one page. The reference seat reads the same documents and points at text; it never interprets.

The runner is a few hundred lines of Python with no model in the path. That is deliberate. Transcription and comparison are mechanical, and a mechanical step can be tested, replayed, and explained to a board. Every register row comes from one row of the approved-inputs file. Status is decided by comparing that row's approved dates against the files in the vault, and by nothing else. A row whose frequency implies a date but has no approved date is not a row yet; it goes to the items-awaiting-input list with the request date and the recipient. Duty language found in the documents but absent from the approved list goes to the same list as a yes-or-no question for counsel.

The reference seat, the piece the client experiences as "Arthur," is retrieval over the client's own documents with a refusal rule in front of it. Ask where the annual report requirement is and it returns the section, the line, and the text. Ask whether a bond call is material and it answers with one sentence, logs the question, and stops. Optionally it also searches our reference shelf, a twelve-volume practitioner's guide, and labels every hit as secondary synthesis to be verified against primary text before use.

4. Synthetic data, and why we could not have built it any other way

We do not have a client's binder to build on, and we would not use one for a public demo if we did. So the demo deal is invented. SYN-HSG-AZ-2025 is a multifamily housing conduit financing for a borrower that does not exist, issued by an authority that does not exist, in a city that does not exist. Every name, amount, and date in it is made up. What is not made up is the shape.

The shape comes from our research program, MUNI-TWIN, which builds a digital twin of conduit issuance. One of its artifacts is a deidentified index of a real closing transcript: eighty-seven entries, page spans, document titles exactly as written, with the borrower, the series, and twenty-six property names substituted out. Three layers, three hashes. The verbatim extract, the deidentified transform, and the domain judgment that collapsed it into fifty-seven node types are kept separate and reproducible.

The rule that makes synthetic data usable

Metadata travels, content does not. A document list, a clause structure, a deadline pattern can leave the source. The text, the numbers, and the names never do.

That index told us which documents a real binder contains and in what order. From it we wrote five synthetic excerpts, a continuing disclosure agreement with sixteen listed events, a loan agreement with quarterly and annual deliverables, an indenture, a tax regulatory agreement with a rebate clock, and a land use restriction agreement with set-aside reporting. The runner was built and tested against them. Internally, the same runner is checked against our own issuer-side book. Publicly, a stranger sees the synthetic deal. A client sees their own documents. Nothing in between.

6. The front door

The public page follows a seven-section skeleton: what this is, what you get, what we never do, who it is for, how it works, the guarantee, how to start. The "never" section is written as the reason to trust the file, not as a disclaimer. The intake form asks the gate question first and alone. Answer no, and the form ends. It collects facts and contacts only; nothing on it invites a legal judgment. Submissions land in the same lead table as every other inquiry, flagged for "entity unclear," "concurrent pre-issuance work," and "no professional named," and the team is emailed at once.

Beside the paid path sits a free one. The DIY kit is four plain-text files: a complete-drop checklist, a register template, the four-code legend, and a skill file a prospect hands to whatever AI assistant they already use. The skill produces a candidate map, quotes every deadline verbatim, and refuses the same questions we refuse. It ends with an explicit line, "end of instructions for the AI," so the sales copy below it is never read as an instruction.

7. The list engine

The same discipline runs the prospect list. The route is issuer or registry, then deal, then obligated person, then sponsor, then a named human. Four of those steps are public record. Conduit issuers publish their transaction lists; one Arizona authority's list yielded one hundred and twenty-seven closed deals in a single afternoon. A research pass on the housing and industrial rows resolved sponsors and websites for twenty-six of thirty, citing a public source on each. A script then applies the qualification rules mechanically: a municipal owner goes to the nurture file, a sponsor with a new issuance pending goes to a holds file with a recheck trigger, a defunct borrower is retired, an unresolved sponsor waits. Nothing enters a send without a source citation.

8. What we deliberately did not build

Left out on purpose
  • A fifth status code, a score, a rating, a colour by importance, or any field that records our assessment of anything.
  • Date computation from deadline formulas.
  • Filing on the MSRB's public filing system, or the word "compliant" anywhere in a deliverable.
  • Materiality determinations, notice drafting, or any pre-issuance work inside this engagement.
  • A DIY calendar. The kit stops at candidates because a calendar without approved dates is a judgment wearing a spreadsheet.

Part II · Op-ed

AI as clerk, not counsel

The bottleneck in post-issuance work was never judgment. It was custody. The value of these tools is in doing the mechanical half flawlessly and refusing the other half out loud.

Every stakeholder in a conduit bond deal has spent twenty years assuming the hard part is the legal part. The advisor structures, counsel opines, the underwriter prices, the trustee holds. Then the deal closes and the promises go into a binder that nobody opens until something goes wrong. The annual report is late because the person who knew the deadline left. The rent roll was sent to the wrong address. The rebate computation was due in year five and nobody put year five on a calendar. None of these failures needed a lawyer to prevent. They needed a clerk who never forgets and never guesses.

That is what these capabilities are. Not artificial intelligence in the sense the industry has been sold. A transcription discipline, a fixed vocabulary, and a refusal rule, wrapped around the documents a borrower already owns.

What changes now, for each seat at the table

The obligated person

One working file. Every undertaking beside the clause that created it, with the evidence for the last one and the date of the next. A calendar with a source. A gap list that says what is missing and stops.

Bond counsel and the dissemination agent

Cleaner inputs. Questions arrive pre-sorted with clause citations, not as a panicked call in June. The approval they give is recorded as data, so the register can show it was theirs.

The trustee

Evidence arrives named by obligation and period. The certificate for section 6.5 for fiscal 2025 is a file called that, not an attachment to a thread.

The conduit issuer

A borrower who keeps a book is a borrower whose problems surface early. The issuer's own post-issuance responsibilities do not change, and the register never speaks for the issuer.

Investors and underwriters

Undertakings that are tracked tend to be kept. The register makes no claim about any filing's status; it makes the keeping cheaper.

The municipal advisor

Not replaced. Handed decisions instead of conclusions. Every judgment question the register refuses is a question the advisor is paid to answer.

Four words, and no fifth. The discipline is in what the vocabulary refuses to say.

The status vocabulary deserves its own paragraph, because it is where most compliance software goes wrong. The temptation is to add a code that means "a professional should look at this." It feels careful. It is the opposite. It partitions the register into the rows we flagged and the rows we did not, and the rows we did not flag now read as cleared. Nobody has to write "compliant" for a reader to infer it. Our four codes are observations about documents: is there a file, does it correspond, can we test it. The routing that a fifth code was reaching for lives in two lists instead, one of requests and one of refusals, and neither says anything about the obligation.

What changes next

Registers that survive the deal's own history. Refundings, fiscal-year changes, successor trustees, and amended undertakings currently break whatever spreadsheet exists. When the approval trail is data, each of those events re-opens exactly the rows it touches, and the items-awaiting-input list tells everyone what has to be re-approved.

Tools that improve without touching anyone's documents. A synthetic deal that carries the shape of a real binder, with none of its content, lets the extractor be built, tested, and stress-tested in public. The deidentified index that made our demo possible is one artifact. A library of them is a training set no client had to contribute to.

A shared vocabulary. Four statuses, a refusal log, and an items-awaiting-input list are simple enough to become a convention between borrowers and their professionals. A trustee who receives files named by obligation and period from ten borrowers will start asking the eleventh for the same.

Agents that keep the book alive, and the people who keep the agents alive. A workflow a borrower runs themselves will drift. Models change. A prompt that worked in June returns something different in December. The free kit we published does the first mile and says so; the maintenance, the approval chasing, the evidence custody, and the re-testing are the work, and they need an owner. That is where a done-with-you or done-for-you service earns its fee, and it is an honest place to earn it.

The limits, stated plainly

  • The approved-input rule is load-bearing. Remove it and the register becomes our determination dressed as the client's.
  • The method is activity-reviewed, not counsel-cleared. That is the correct phrase and it stays until a lawyer signs.
  • We never file. We never say "compliant." We never replace counsel, the advisor, or the dissemination agent.
  • Nothing here is a claim about the market. It is a claim about custody.

The obligation book is the product. Everything else is filing.

Appendix · Six terms

Obligated personThe private borrower behind a conduit bond. The party that actually repays and actually promises.
Continuing disclosure agreementThe undertaking, made at closing, to provide annual information and notices of certain events for the life of the bonds.
Dissemination agentThe party appointed to make those filings. The register never becomes one.
Listed eventsThe enumerated events, sixteen in current form, that trigger a notice. Whether one has occurred and whether it is material are judgments.
Approved inputA row, clause, or date that a professional has confirmed in writing. The only thing that can enter a register.
Refusal logThe record of every judgment question put to us, the date, and where it was routed. Questions go in; answers do not.
