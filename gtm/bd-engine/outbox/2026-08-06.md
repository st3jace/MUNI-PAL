- [ ] **Post — draft:**

> Most people describe a conduit bond deal as a sequence of stages. More useful framing: some of those steps are revolving doors, and four of them are one-way. Knowing which is which changes what you do first.

> **Revolving (cheap to redo, and you will):** the project budget, the pro forma, the draft sources-and-uses, who you're talking to about credit enhancement. These churn constantly right up to pricing. Operators lose enormous energy trying to perfect them early. Don't. They're supposed to move.

> **One-way door #1 — the official-intent resolution.** In most structures, money you spend on the project *before* there's a formal declaration of intent on the record generally can't be reimbursed out of bond proceeds later. There are specific windows and exceptions, and your bond counsel will give you the exact rule for your deal — but the shape of it is fixed: dollars spent on the wrong side of that date are usually just your dollars. Operators pre-funding site work while "still exploring financing" are making an irreversible decision they don't know they're making.

> **One-way door #2 — the public approval / hearing.** The project as *described* in that process is the project you've bought approval for. Materially change the scope, the size, or the use afterward and you're not editing a document — you're re-running a step with a calendar attached to somebody else's meeting schedule. Get the description right, and get it wide enough to survive normal design evolution.

> **One-way door #3 — the offering document goes out.** Before it prints, your disclosure is a draft. After, it's a statement investors relied on. The standard you're held to changes, and so does the cost of "we'll clean that up later." Everything vague you left in the file is now a vague thing you said publicly.

> **One-way door #4 — closing.** Continuing disclosure, covenant compliance, reporting — these don't end at closing, they *start*. The operator who treated the deal as a fundraise finds out in month four that they signed up for an ongoing obligation with no one assigned to it.

> The practical version: the reversible parts of a bond deal are the parts everyone works on first, and the irreversible parts are the ones that arrive as surprises. Front-load the doors. Let the spreadsheets churn.
