## Content (1 post) — Angle 2: Readiness myth-busting

*Longest-unused usable angle (last ran 07-07; recent window cycled 3→2→1→5→6→3). Fresh myth trio, distinct from 06-23, 06-30, and 07-07. Honest positioning, no client numbers. Angle 4/proof-story still blocked by empty proof library.*

- [ ] **Post draft:**

> **Three things operators believe about bond finance that quietly disqualify them — and the real bar underneath each.**
>
> **Myth 1: "Bonds are for hospitals, universities, and governments — not a company like ours."**
> The reality: conduit and exempt-facility financing routinely fund for-profit operators — dairy processors, recyclers, ag operations, housing developers, waste and industrial facilities. The question a conduit issuer asks isn't "are you a nonprofit," it's "does the project fit an eligible category and can you carry the disclosure." Plenty of private companies clear that bar every quarter.
>
> **Myth 2: "There's a minimum deal size and we're too small."**
> The reality: the "minimum" people imagine is usually borrowed from the corporate-bond world. Conduit and IRB deals span a wide range, and small single-project financings close regularly. Size screens you out far less often than an incomplete file does.
>
> **Myth 3: "We have to be fully permitted and shovel-ready before we can even start."**
> The reality: readiness and disclosure work runs *in parallel* with the rest of your development timeline — and starting it early is the advantage, not a prerequisite you have to finish first. Operators who wait until they're "ready" to get ready are the ones who miss a board cycle and lose a quarter.
>
> The common thread: the gap is almost never eligibility or size. It's a *readiness story a lender trusts* + *a disclosure package that survives scrutiny* + *someone who's run the process before.* Most operators have one of the three.
>
> If you're sitting on a facility project and you've quietly assumed one of the three myths above rules you out — it probably doesn't. Happy to walk anyone through where they actually stand, free.
