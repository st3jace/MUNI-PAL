## Content — 1 post (angle 5: checklist excerpt — FRESH cut: "the readiness checklist, by who owns each line")

*Prior angle-5 cuts: 06-25 (the 5-point check a lender runs), 07-02 (the 5 questions a lender asks), 07-09 (the 5-line pre-check). Today's fresh cut = ownership/accountability framing: the checklist reframed as "whose desk does each line die on."*

- [ ] **POST:**

> Most "bond readiness checklists" fail because they list *what* is needed and skip *who owns it*. A line item with no owner is a line item that stalls. Here's the 5-line version with the owner named:
>
> 1. **3 years of clean, explainable financials** — owner: your controller/CFO. Not just clean; *explainable* to someone who's never seen your business.
> 2. **A repeatable project story** — owner: you (the principal). Why this facility, why now, why it pencils. If only one person can tell it, it's not ready.
> 3. **The right conduit named** — owner: whoever knows the issuer landscape. Wrong issuer = wrong-shaped deal from day one.
> 4. **A disclosure owner** — owner: a single named human accountable for the paper. This is the line most operators leave blank, and it's where the calendar goes to die.
> 5. **A process operator who has closed one before** — owner: someone who's run the gauntlet. Experience here is not a luxury; it's the difference between a 6-month file and an 18-month one.
>
> Score yourself 0–5: one point per line that has a *named owner* today, not just a good intention. Most operators I meet are at a 2 or 3 — and every missing point is a specific person to assign, not a wall to climb.
>
> The gap is never "you're not ready." It's "line 4 has no owner." Name the owner, close the gap.
>
> *(We run this exact checklist on live engagements — it's the system we use, not a client result.)*
