## Content (1 post) — Angle 2: Readiness Myth-Busting

**Topic: "Three things you've been told you need before a bond — that you don't."** (Honest positioning — describes the real thresholds, cites no client numbers.)

> Operators talk themselves out of conduit/tax-exempt bond financing on advice that isn't actually true. Three of the most common myths:
>
> **Myth 1: "You need a credit rating first."** For most conduit and IRB structures, no. The real threshold is a *disclosure package that survives underwriter scrutiny* — the rating conversation, when it happens at all, comes after that, not before.
>
> **Myth 2: "You need to be a big institution."** A dairy-processing plant, an aluminum recycler, a 200-unit housing developer — all clearing conduit deals right now, all in public filings. The category is far wider than "hospitals and universities." If you operate a facility, you're probably more eligible than you've been told.
>
> **Myth 3: "You're two years out."** Sometimes true. But "not ready" almost never means the *project* is wrong — it means the readiness story and the paper aren't assembled yet. That's a months-of-work problem, not a years-of-waiting problem, and the timeline shrinks fast once someone owns the document list.
>
> The pattern under all three: operators are told they're further from fundable than they are, by people who aren't paid to map the actual gap. We built the system we use to map it on live engagements. If you keep hearing "you're not ready" and it's never come with a checklist — that's the conversation I give away free.
>
> Which of these three have you been told? 👇
