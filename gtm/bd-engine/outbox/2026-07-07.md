## Content — 1 post (angle 2: readiness myth-busting)

*Fresh myth set, distinct from 06-23 (rating-required / collateral-like-a-bank / issuer-beauty-contest) and 06-30. Honest positioning; no client numbers (proof library empty).*

> **Three things operators believe about bond finance that quietly cost them a deal.**
>
> **Myth 1: "We have to be profitable / investment-grade first."** A lot of conduit financings fund projects that aren't fully operating yet — the deal is underwritten on the project and the structure, not just a trailing P&L. Waiting to be "clean enough" is often waiting for a bar that was never the actual bar.
>
> **Myth 2: "This takes years."** The market piece rarely takes years. What takes years — when it does — is the *disclosure and readiness gap*: the paper that survives scrutiny, the story a lender can trust, an operator who's run the process before. Close that gap and the timeline is usually months. Most people are measuring the wrong clock.
>
> **Myth 3: "We need a big-name national underwriter to be taken seriously."** The logo matters less than most operators think. A credible readiness story and the right conduit relationship carry more weight than a marquee name attached to an unprepared borrower.
>
> The pattern underneath all three: operators disqualify themselves on assumptions, not on the actual rules. We built the system we use to map where a project *actually* stands — and the first readiness conversation is free.
>
> *(If you keep getting told "you're not ready," it's worth knowing whether that's true or just unexamined.)*
