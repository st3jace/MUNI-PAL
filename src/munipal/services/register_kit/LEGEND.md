# The four statuses, and the two lists that are not statuses

Once your counsel or dissemination agent has approved the obligation map in writing, each approved row can carry **one of exactly four words**. Nothing else. No score, no rating, no colour by importance.

| Status | Meaning | Comes from |
|---|---|---|
| `filed` | An evidence file matching the approved undertaking and period is in the vault | A written client fact, or professional input |
| `not filed` | The due date is in the future. Nothing is expected yet | The approved due date |
| `evidence missing` | The due date has passed and no matching file is in the vault | A mechanical comparison of the approved row against the files you supplied. It says nothing about what happened outside those files |
| `not testable` | The undertaking has no calendar date (event-driven, conditional, on request, standing) and no event or request was supplied | The approved row |

## Why there is no fifth status

It is tempting to add "needs a professional" or "to be determined". Do not. Marking a row that way is itself a determination about how legally uncertain that obligation is, and the rows you did not mark then read as cleared. That is how "we track your obligations" drifts into "you are compliant" without anyone writing the word.

The two things people reach for a fifth status to do live somewhere else:

**Items awaiting input.** A row whose obligation has no written professional input yet is not a register row. It sits on a separate list: what was asked, of whom, on what date. Sorted by request date only.

**Refusal log.** Every judgment question anyone asks — "is this material?", "are we late?", "do we need a notice?" — is logged with the date and who it was routed to. It is not answered. The log records a question declined, not a property of the obligation.

## Words that never appear in a register

compliant · compliance · in good standing · cleared · on track · at risk · late (as a conclusion) · material (as a conclusion) · bond-ready
