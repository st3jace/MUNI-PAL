- [ ] **Post — draft:**

> Scroll a stretch of conduit issuance and a pattern shows up that operators rarely notice: **a meaningful share of the borrowers have been there before.**

> LDG's Redwood apartments financed in 2020, then again in 2024. Conduit programs running numbered series year after year — 2024-1, 2025-1, 2026-1 — with the same names recurring underneath them. These are public filings; anyone can go look.

> Here's why that matters if you're doing your first deal.

> **You are not doing the same thing they are doing.** You're doing a *project.* They're running a *program.* Same paperwork, entirely different economics — because the second time through, most of the file already exists, most of the questions have already been answered once, and the people on the other side of the table already know who they're dealing with.

> Which means your first deal has two outputs, not one. The obvious one is the money. The one nobody schedules is **the file** — the entity chart, the authority record, the multi-year financials on a consistent basis, the site-control paper, the disclosure infrastructure. Built one way, that file is an asset you refresh. Built the other way, it's a pile of documents assembled under deadline pressure that nobody can locate in three years.

> The tell is what happens right after closing. Continuing disclosure and covenant reporting begin the day the deal ends, and on a first deal there is very often **no one assigned to them** — counsel's engagement is finished, the advisors have moved on, and the operator's team goes back to operating. Two years later the record has gaps, and the second deal gets underwritten as if it were the first one.

> So the practical read on the repeat-issuer pattern isn't "those are the big guys." It's that **the difference between a project and a program is mostly decided by decisions you make in the first ninety days after closing**, when the pressure is off and nobody's asking.

> If you're heading into deal #1, one question is worth more than most of the rest: *who owns this file after we close?*
