## Content (1 post) — angle 5: Checklist Excerpt (give it away free; no fabricated numbers)

*Rotated off yesterday's angle-6 market-signal post.*

> **The 5 things a lender needs to see before "you're not bond-ready" turns into "let's go."**
>
> Most operators hear "not ready" and assume it means *too small* or *too risky*. Almost never true. It usually means one of these five is missing — and the fix is paperwork, not size:
>
> 1. **A credible use-of-funds story** — what the money builds, why now, and how it gets repaid, in language a lender's credit committee accepts (not your pitch deck).
> 2. **Clean, current financials** — 3 years if you have them, with the related-party stuff explained *before* they ask.
> 3. **A disclosure package that survives scrutiny** — the official-statement inputs: project description, risk factors, sources & uses. This is where most deals stall for months.
> 4. **The right conduit issuer identified** — an IDA or finance authority whose mandate fits your project. Most operators don't know this door exists, let alone which one to knock on.
> 5. **Someone who's run the gauntlet before** — bond finance is a process, not a product. The first time through, the unknowns are the expensive part.
>
> Have one of the five and you feel stuck. Have all five and the same deal that got a "no" gets a "when can you close."
>
> We run this readiness system live on our own IDA engagement. If you've got a facility project and you keep hearing "not ready," I'll walk you through where you actually stand — free, no pitch.
