## Content (1 post) — *angle 3: Hidden-cost spotlight (longest-unused usable angle; last ran 06-22 — fresh friction this time)*

- [ ] **Post — "The most expensive thing about a bond deal isn't the fees. It's the calendar."**

> Operators planning a facility financing almost always budget for the visible costs: counsel, issuance fees, the rate. Almost none budget for the one that actually hurts — the **approval calendar**.

> A conduit issuer's board doesn't meet on demand. It meets on a cycle — monthly, sometimes quarterly. Miss the packet deadline for one meeting and your whole project slides to the next window. Two missed windows and a "spring close" is a "fall close." That slip isn't a fee line; it's carrying costs, a held construction price, a lender's rate lock expiring, a use-it-or-lose-it incentive.

> Here's the part nobody tells first-time operators: **you don't lose the window because you're not eligible. You lose it because the readiness package wasn't assembled in time to make the agenda.** The deadline is knowable months out. The work to hit it is knowable. It just rarely gets sequenced backward from the board date.

> Map the issuer's meeting calendar first. Work backward to every packet deadline. Then assemble to *that* clock, not to "when we get to it." The operators who close on schedule aren't faster — they started from the calendar.

> *(We run this backward-from-the-board-date sequencing on our own live engagements. No client numbers here — just the pattern.)*
