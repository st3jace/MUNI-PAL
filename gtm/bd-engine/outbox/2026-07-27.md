- [ ] **Post draft:**
  > Nobody warns first-time bond borrowers about the cast list.
  >
  > You start a conduit deal with two parties: you and the issuer. By closing you have eight or nine. And here's the part operators miss — **every party you add brings a document request, not a shortcut.**
  >
  > Roughly who shows up, and what each one actually wants from *you*:
  >
  > • **Issuer (the IDA/authority)** — wants to know the project serves its statutory purpose. Asks for: the project narrative, jobs/impact facts, an inducement request.
  > • **Bond counsel** — wants the deal to be legally tax-exempt. Asks for: entity documents, ownership chart, use-of-proceeds detail, site control.
  > • **Municipal advisor / placement agent** — wants a story the market will buy. Asks for: financial history, projections, and the assumptions underneath them.
  > • **Underwriter or purchaser** — wants to know what happens if the project underperforms. Asks for: audited financials, a feasibility or market study, management background.
  > • **Trustee** — wants operational mechanics. Asks for: flow of funds, covenant tests, reporting schedule.
  > • **Your own counsel and auditor** — want everything above to be internally consistent.
  >
  > Notice what didn't happen: none of them handed you a checklist at the start. Each one arrives on their own timeline and asks for their own slice, and the operator becomes a full-time document router — usually while still running the actual business.
  >
  > The teams that move fastest do one unglamorous thing: they assemble the file **before** the cast arrives, so each new party is answered from a shelf instead of a scramble. Same documents either way. Completely different calendar.
  >
  > If you're a party or two into a deal and already feel behind — you're not behind. You're just doing it in the order the deal handed you instead of the order that works.
