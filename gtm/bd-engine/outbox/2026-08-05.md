- [ ] **Post — draft:**

> Three things I hear from facility operators that sound like caution and are actually a handoff of the wrong question.
>
> **"We need to line up an issuer first."**
> The issuer is not the gating decision. The gating decision is which category your project falls into — because that's what sets your volume-cap exposure, your public-approval path, and what your proceeds are allowed to touch. Operators spend months shopping conduits before anyone has confirmed the bucket. The bucket is upstream of the issuer, and it's usually answerable in an afternoon.
>
> **"Our bank passed, so bonds are out."**
> A bank and a conduit deal are not looking at the same object. A bank is underwriting your balance sheet, your recourse, and your relationship. A conduit financing is underwriting the project's revenue and its security structure, for a different buyer with different requirements. A bank "no" is one lens returning one answer. It's real information — it is not a verdict on a financing you never applied for.
>
> **"We've retained bond counsel, so the process is being run."**
> This is the expensive one. Bond counsel opines on validity and tax treatment. That's the job, and it's essential. But counsel will not chase your auditor, will not own your document calendar, and will not decide what goes in your file. In most stalled deals the empty seat isn't a professional nobody hired — it's the operator-side person whose only job is running the process. Hiring more advisors doesn't fill it. It usually makes it more obvious.
>
> The pattern under all three: each one takes a question the operator has to answer and hands it to someone whose job description doesn't include it.
>
> We built the system we run on our own live engagements to answer exactly these — the category, the actual gap, and who owns each open line. Happy to walk anyone through where they stand, free, whether or not it goes anywhere.
