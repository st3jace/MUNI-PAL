**Angle 5 · Checklist excerpt · FRESH cut — the half of the checklist you can finish before you have a project.** *(Distinct from every prior angle-5 cut: 06-25 what-the-lender-checks, 07-02 the-questions-a-lender-asks, 07-09 the-pre-check, 07-16 who-owns-each-line. Today's axis is **prerequisite-independence** — splitting the readiness checklist into entity-level lines that require no project at all vs project-level lines that can't start until one exists. The point: most operators wait for the project to begin work that never needed the project. Honest positioning, no client numbers, proof library empty.)*

- [ ] **Post draft:**
  > Most operators think bond readiness starts when the project does.
  >
  > It doesn't. Split the readiness checklist in two and about half of it has **nothing to do with your project.**
  >
  > **Lines you can finish today, with no project, no site, no deal:**
  > • **Entity documents that are actually current** — formation docs, amendments, good standing, the operating agreement that matches how you really operate. Not the version in the drawer.
  > • **An ownership chart a stranger can read** — every parent, sub, and member, with percentages. This one item stalls more deals than any financial issue.
  > • **Financial statements at the right altitude** — reviewed or audited, consistent year to year, with the story behind the anomalies written down while you still remember it.
  > • **Governance that can act** — who signs, who authorizes, what the board actually has to vote on and how far out it meets.
  >
  > **Lines that genuinely require a project:** site control, use of proceeds, the feasibility or market study, project budget and sources-and-uses, environmental and permitting.
  >
  > Here's the trap. The first list takes weeks of unglamorous work and can be done **any time**. The second list is urgent, visible, and gets all the attention. So operators wait for a project to start, then discover they're doing both lists at once — under a clock, in front of eight parties who each want a piece of the first list before they'll look at the second.
  >
  > The operators who move fast aren't faster. They finished list one during the quiet stretch, when nobody was waiting on them.
  >
  > If your project is 12 months out, that's not "too early to prepare." That's the only unpressured window you get.
  >
  > *(This is the readiness system we run on our own live engagements — sharing the checklist, not selling it.)*
