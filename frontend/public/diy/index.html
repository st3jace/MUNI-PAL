The one rule

The skill produces candidates. A candidate becomes an obligation only when your bond counsel, municipal advisor, or dissemination agent approves the row, the clause, and the due dates, in writing. Dates are never computed from the document text, by you or by the AI.

That rule is the whole method. Everything else is filing.

What the kit cannot do

This is the honest part. The kit does the first mile. These are the jobs that keep the book alive, and none of them is a prompt:

  • Getting the approval. Someone sends the map to your professionals, chases the written answers, and records them so every row has approval behind it.
  • Keeping the calendar honest. A refunding, a fiscal-year change, a successor trustee. Each one re-opens rows that someone has to get re-approved.
  • The evidence vault. Files arrive by email, misnamed, in the wrong folder. Someone binds each one to its row, quarter after quarter, and writes "evidence missing" when it is missing.
  • Maintaining the workflow itself. Agent workflows drift. Models change. A prompt that worked in June returns something different in December. Someone has to re-test it and notice when the output quietly changed shape.
  • Standing behind it. A process guarantee and a refusal log you can hand to your board.
