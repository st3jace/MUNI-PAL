## 2. What the roadmap actually is

The Acquisition.com roadmap maps a business from $0 to $100M across **10 stages**, each defined by **headcount** (0 → 500 employees). Each stage breaks into **8 functions** — product, marketing, sales, customer service, IT, recruiting, HR, finance — and for each stage names exactly **one binding constraint** and **one bottom-line move to graduate**.

Their deliberate design choice: they indexed by *number of people*, not revenue, because organizational structure is more consistent across businesses than revenue is. A 35-person restaurant and a 35-person software company look structurally alike even at a 50× revenue gap.

**That choice is the thing we have to translate.**

## 3. The core reframe for a solopreneur

> **Their hidden variable:** how many *humans* perform the 8 functions.
> **Your hidden variable:** how many of the 8 functions you've *systematized* so they run without your live attention.

You don't graduate stages by hiring. You graduate by converting a function from "Stephen does it live, in the moment, when he remembers" into "a system/agent runs it on a cadence and only escalates exceptions."

This yields a sharp, uncomfortable diagnosis:

- The roadmap's **late stages (6–9)** are dominated by back-office machinery — IT, recruiting, HR, finance ops. **You already operate at that level of sophistication** (agent harness, scheduled tasks, 16 platforms, the Visibility Dashboard, the Hermes control plane).
- The roadmap's **early stages (1–2)** are entirely the four **revenue functions** — offer, marketing, sales, customer success. **This is where solopreneurs stall, and it is the least-systematized part of your stack.**

**Your constraint is inverted from where you've invested.** You built the factory before the revenue engine that justifies it. This framework points the factory's machinery at the revenue engine.

## 4. The two-product architecture

You chose Pilot Navigation **and** Muni-Pal/BFMS together. That pairing is not two businesses — it is one business at two points on the roadmap, and the relationship between them is the whole strategy:

| | **Pilot Navigation** | **Muni-Pal / BFMS** |
|---|---|---|
| Roadmap analogue | Stage 1–2 offer: *valuable, deliberately unscalable* | Stage 5 "Productize": *the scalable second product* |
| What it is | High-touch advisory engagement ($15–75K+ per engagement, $20K/yr subscription) | The platform/SaaS that productizes what the advisory work taught you |
| Binding constraint | **Bond Strategist capacity** (20–35 hrs/week per Tier-2 engagement) | Feature toggles, pipeline of qualified operators |
| Role in the loop | Generates proof, learns the real hidden costs, funds the platform | Captures the repeatable parts of the service so they stop consuming Strategist hours |

**The strategic move (straight from the Stage 5 "Productize" logic):** the service is where you discover what's valuable; the platform is where you make the repeatable 80% of it scalable, so each new engagement consumes fewer Bond Strategist hours than the last. Productizing is *how you relieve your own binding constraint.*

The Stage 1–2 warning applies to you directly: **builders make the offer scalable before they make it valuable.** Resist platform-building that outruns proven advisory value. Let the service teach the platform what to automate.

## 5. The BD loop — your revenue engine

The four revenue functions are not a list; they are a closed loop. Stages 3–9 only ever add *capacity* to a loop that must first work at all:

```
        ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │                                             │
        ▼                                             │
   [1] OFFER ──► [2] MARKETING ──► [3] SALES ──► [4] CUSTOMER SUCCESS
  make it       consistent        one script    deliver value +
  valuable      lead flow         run every     harvest proof
  (find the     (Rule of 100)     time          (Epiphany Bridge)
  hidden cost)                    (CLOSER)              │
        ▲                                               │
        └──────────── proof feeds back ─────────────────┘
```

Deliver value → harvest proof → proof powers marketing → marketing feeds sales → sales delivers value. **That loop *is* business development.** Build it before building anything that merely scales it.

## 6. The four revenue functions (instantiated for bond operators)

### [1] Offer — make it valuable before scalable

**Value Equation:** `(Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)`

For a healthcare/muni bond operator the variables are concrete:
- **Dream outcome:** access to bond financing they were told they couldn't get / closing the facility deal.
- **Perceived likelihood:** raised by *proof* (closed deals, named comparable operators) — this is your weakest input today and your highest-leverage one.
- **Time delay:** time-to-readiness, time-to-close. Muni-Pal/BFMS exists to compress this.
- **Effort & sacrifice:** the work the operator would otherwise do themselves — readiness assessment, disclosure prep, document assembly. Every BFMS feature that removes a step raises offer value.

**Find the hidden costs.** The non-monetary reasons an operator says no. Ask two questions literally:
- *"What would it take?"* (assume the purchase, work backward to features)
- *"If this were $X to get you to a closeable deal, what would you need to see to be excited to move forward?"* (price already set, surfaces the real objection)

**Charge premium** so you can afford to remove friction. Their argument applies precisely to a 20–35 hr/week Tier-2 engagement: premium pricing is what *lets* you deliver the unscalable value.

### [2] Marketing — consistency beats cleverness

**The diagnosis:** inconsistent lead flow is *you* being inconsistent. As a solopreneur this is the single most common failure mode and the single best thing to systematize.

**The Rule of 100** — a daily input quota you hold regardless of how full the pipeline feels. Stop advertising when you're busy and you'll wish you hadn't a month later. Run it across the **Core Four**:
1. **Warm outreach** — operators/intermediaries who know you (1-to-1, private).
2. **Cold outreach** — targeted lists, LinkedIn filters, referrals-of-referrals.
3. **Content (1-to-many)** — ~100 min/day: make the post *and* spend outreach on people who engaged + best-comment on others' threads in your audience's spaces.
4. **Paid ads** — time is the constraint, not money: hooks, creative, testing.

**Proof > Promise.** Never lead with claims; lead with results. Don't scale selling until you have ~10 strong proof artifacts. Weight proof by: in-person > virtual, recent > old, visual/show > tell, *similar-to-the-prospect* > generic, specific numbers > vague. For bond work: a closed deal for a comparable operator type, with the dollar figure, beats any brochure. **This is why the service comes first — it manufactures the proof the marketing engine runs on.**

### [3] Sales — one script, run every time

**CLOSER:** Clarify why they engaged → Label the problem/plan → Overview past pains & attempts → **S**ell the vacation (not the plane flight) → Explain away concerns → Reinforce the decision.

Most of the call is *questions that increase deprivation*: where are you now / where do you want to be / what's stopped you / what have you tried / how'd that work. Then a tight **three-pillar pitch** (the 3 things an operator needs to be financeable), one line + analogy each, ~2–3 minutes. The five concern buckets to pre-load answers for: **time, stall/indecision, money, preference, authority/decision-maker.**

Track the funnel on a sheet from day one (Stage 4's "spreadsheet, not CRM" move): **contact → schedule → show → offer → close → cash collected.** Raise price 20% every ~5 closes until throughput tells you the ceiling.

### [4] Customer Success — two scripts: rescue and harvest

**Rescue (HEAR), for upset clients:** **H**ear them out fully → **E**mpathize and escalate *past* their upset (the "angry boat" — only one person can be in it) → **A**pologize and take accountability → **R**esolve *with* them, offering options. Never cower from a hard conversation; confront it.

**Harvest (Epiphany Bridge), for happy clients:** capture the testimonial as a journey — internal struggle (rock bottom before) → external metrics (the objective before/after numbers) → why they chose *you* over alternatives. This output is the raw material for Function [2]. **The loop closes here:** every successful engagement must end with a harvested proof artifact, or marketing starves.

## 7. The inverted-constraint diagnosis

Run each function through "is this systematized, or does it depend on Stephen in the moment?"

| Function | Current state (likely) | Binding constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Offer | Defined (tiers, pricing) ✓ | Hidden-cost discovery is ad hoc |
| **Marketing** | **Not systematized** ✗ | **No consistent lead-gen cadence — the gap** |
| Sales | Ad hoc | No standing script + KPI sheet |
| Customer Success | High-touch, manual | **Bond Strategist hours (20–35/wk per engagement)** |
| IT / Ops / Finance | **Over-built** ✓✓ | Not the constraint |

**Two real constraints, in priority order:**
1. **Lead flow** (Marketing) — there is no revenue to scale without it. Fix first.
2. **Bond Strategist capacity** (delivery) — caps how many engagements you can hold at once. This is what Muni-Pal/BFMS productization is *for*: move repeatable Strategist work into the platform so each engagement costs fewer hours.

Everything you've already built (Stages 6–9 ops) is not the constraint and should not absorb more attention until these two are addressed.

## 8. Systems to build (agentize the BD functions)

The translation that's genuinely yours — for each revenue function, the question is not "who do I hire" but "what agent/system runs this so it doesn't depend on my attention." You already own the scheduling, notification, and agent infrastructure (EDRS checkpoints, the weekly bond briefing, scheduled tasks). Point the same machinery at revenue:

| Function | System to build | Reuses |
|---|---|---|
| Offer | Hidden-cost interview template + Value-Equation scorer run on every prospect call → outputs offer revisions | Skill/prompt pattern |
| **Marketing** | **Rule-of-100 engine** — scheduled agent that drafts the day's Core-Four outreach/content and *holds the quota accountable* (daily push, weekly tally). **Highest leverage. Build first.** | Scheduled tasks + Telegram/push (cf. EDRS daily checkpoint) |
| Sales | CLOSER call-prep + objection library + funnel KPI sheet (contact→…→cash) auto-updated | Sheet + agent |
| Customer Success | Testimonial-harvest agent (Epiphany Bridge prompts fired at engagement milestones) that files proof into a library the marketing engine draws from | Client-launch-protocol hooks |
| Delivery (the capacity constraint) | Each repeatable Bond Strategist task → a BFMS feature or agent, measured by *Strategist-hours-per-engagement falling over time* | BFMS roadmap |

The marketing engine and the harvest agent are two ends of the same loop — build them as a pair and the loop runs itself.

## 9. Sequencing — what to do next

1. **Stand up the Rule-of-100 engine** for Pilot Navigation lead gen. Define the Core-Four daily quota, wire a scheduled agent + daily push, start a weekly tally. *Nothing else matters until lead flow is consistent.* — **BUILT 2026-06-10: see [`../bd-engine/`](../bd-engine/README.md), scheduled weekdays 7:55am.**
2. **Instrument the sales funnel** — the 6-column KPI sheet, populated from real conversations. You can't manage what you don't measure.
3. **Harvest proof from existing/early engagements** (SVIDA-class reference work counts) via the Epiphany Bridge, into a proof library. This back-fills Function [2]'s ammunition.
4. **Pick the single highest-hour repeatable Strategist task** and productize it into BFMS. Measure Strategist-hours-per-engagement before/after. Repeat. This is how you graduate the capacity constraint.
5. Only then expand channels / tiers / Skool funnel — Stage 4–5 moves that assume a working loop.

**One-line objective:** *Make lead flow consistent and proof systematic; then let the platform eat the Bond Strategist's repeatable hours.* Everything else is downstream.
