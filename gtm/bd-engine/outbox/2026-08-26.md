- [ ] **Post draft:**

  > Operators pay for a lot of things in a bond financing. Here is something they routinely do not use, and it costs nothing.
  >
  > **Every conduit deal that has closed leaves a public file.** The official statement, the continuing-disclosure filings, the material-event notices. It is on EMMA, it is free, there is no login, and it does not care who you are. A facility very much like yours has almost certainly already been financed, and the whole story of how is sitting there.
  >
  > Almost nobody in the operator's seat reads one before they start. They read summaries. They read what an advisor tells them. They very rarely open the actual document for a deal that resembles theirs.
  >
  > **Three things to look at when you do. This is the whole method.**
  >
  > **1. The covenants — read them as a description of your future Tuesdays.** Not as legal text. Somebody agreed to report certain things on a certain rhythm, to hold certain ratios, to get permission before certain moves. That is a job, and it will be somebody's job at your organisation for as long as the bonds are outstanding. Reading it now tells you what you are actually signing up to operate, which is a completely different question from whether you can close.
  >
  > **2. The sources-and-uses table — read it for what the money did *not* go to.** It lists what the proceeds paid for. The gap between the project you picture and the line items in that table is where surprises live. Reserve funds, capitalized interest, costs of issuance. None of it is hidden. It is just rarely looked at before it becomes a number in your own deal.
  >
  > **3. The risk-factors section — read it as a list of the questions you will be asked.** This is the one I would push hardest. Whoever wrote that section had to write down, in public, every way the deal could go wrong. Those are the same concerns that will show up as diligence requests on your file. It is, functionally, the exam paper published in advance.
  >
  > **Why this matters more than it sounds:** an hour of this changes the questions you ask. Operators who have read one comparable file stop asking what a covenant is and start asking what a covenant will cost them to live with. That shift is visible to everyone in the room, and it happens before you have hired anybody.
  >
  > **The give-away, free, use it today:** find one closed deal in your sector — a facility of roughly your type, roughly your size. Open the official statement. Read only those three parts. Ignore the rest of the document; most of it is not for you.
  >
  > You will not become an expert in an afternoon. You will stop being a tourist, and those are not the same thing.
  >
  > *(We run the operator-side readiness and disclosure system on our own live engagements. The above is generic to conduit finance; documents, disclosure practice and issuer processes vary considerably by state, sector and issuer, and this reflects no client's file.
