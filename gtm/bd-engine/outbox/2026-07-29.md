**Angle 6 · Market signal · FRESH cut — the door got wider; the bar didn't move.** *(Distinct from every prior angle-6 cut: 06-19 / 06-26 / 07-03 / 07-10 sector-diversity, 07-17 geography-of-borrowers. Today's axis is **who issues** — the visible consolidation of conduit issuance into a few high-volume national platforms (PFA in Wisconsin, NFA in New Hampshire, FDFC in Florida) alongside the local IDAs, and what that changes for an operator: access stopped being the scarce thing, and a complete file became the scarce thing. All references are public EMMA filings. Honest positioning, no client numbers, proof library still empty.)*

- [ ] **Post draft:**
  > Look at a year of conduit issuance and one thing jumps out: a handful of national issuing platforms now appear on deal after deal, across state lines, next to the local authorities that have always been there.
  >
  > Most operators read that as good news, and it is. It means the old assumption — *"if my local authority doesn't run a program, I don't have a path"* — is mostly obsolete. There are more doors than there used to be.
  >
  > But here's what the same trend does to you on the other side of the door.
  >
  > **A high-volume issuing platform is not a partner. It's a queue.** It exists to process complete, standard-shaped submissions efficiently. It is not staffed to sit with you and figure out what your ownership structure actually is, or why your 2023 financials don't tie to your 2024 ones, or who at your organization has authority to sign.
  >
  > So the scarce resource moved. It used to be **access** — knowing an authority, being on someone's list. Now access is comparatively easy and the scarce thing is **being ready to use it.** The operators who lose time now aren't the ones who can't find an issuer. They're the ones who found one and then spent four months assembling what should have already existed.
  >
  > Two practical consequences:
  >
  > **1. "No local program" is no longer a reason to stop.** It's a reason to ask which conduit structures reach your project — and what each one requires at intake.
  >
  > **2. A wider door raises the cost of an incomplete file, it doesn't lower it.** When the process is standardized, being non-standard is expensive. Nobody's going to hand-hold you through it, because the platform's whole economic model is that it doesn't have to.
  >
  > The honest summary: the market made it easier to get to the table and did nothing to make it easier to sit at it.
  >
  > *(This is the readiness system we run on our own live engagements. Issuance patterns above are from public EMMA filings — anyone can look.)*
