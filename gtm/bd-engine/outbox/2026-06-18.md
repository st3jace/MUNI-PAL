## Content (1 post) — angle 5: readiness checklist excerpt (give it away free, honest positioning)

**Topic: "5 things a lender actually checks before your tax-exempt bond — and the 2 that stall most operators."**

> "You're not bond-ready" is the most expensive sentence an operator hears, because it's almost never explained. Here's the short version of what's actually behind it. Before a conduit/tax-exempt deal closes, the people underwriting it want to see, roughly:
>
> 1. **A credit story that survives a bad quarter** — not your best year, your worst plausible one.
> 2. **Disclosure that matches how the business really runs** — financials, contracts, and operations that reconcile to each other without a scramble.
> 3. **Covenants you can actually live with** — debt-service coverage and reporting terms you'll still hit in year 3, not just at closing.
> 4. **A clean use-of-proceeds and project narrative** — what the money builds, on what timeline, with what fallback.
> 5. **A process operator who's run the gauntlet** — someone accountable for the paper, not five vendors pointing at each other.
>
> In practice the first stall is almost always **#2 (disclosure that reconciles)** and the second is **#3 (covenants you can live with)** — the two that look fine at closing and bite under stress. The project is rarely the problem. The readiness underneath it is.
>
