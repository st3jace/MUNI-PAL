*Prior angle-2 cuts, for the record — this one is organized differently from all of them: 06-23 rating-first / big-institution-only / two-years-out · 06-30 rating-required / collateral-like-a-bank / issuer-beauty-contest · 07-07 must-be-profitable / takes-years / big-name-underwriter · 07-14 nonprofits-only / too-small / must-be-permitted · 07-25 team-first / prior-issuance / eligible≠ready · 08-05 three myths that outsource a question the operator has to own. Those sorted myths by content or by whose job it is. This one sorts them by **why the belief never gets corrected** — each one is sustained not by evidence but by the absence of it.*

- [ ] **Post draft:**

  > Three things operators believe about bond financing that aren't true. What's interesting isn't that they're wrong — it's *why* nobody ever corrects them. Each one survives because the evidence against it exists and never reaches you.
  >
  > **1. "Nobody like us does this."**
  > You've never seen an operator your size do a conduit deal, so you assume it doesn't happen at your size. But consider what you'd actually have to see for the belief to break. Large financings get announced. Mid-size facility deals close quietly, get filed, and nobody issues a press release about their disclosure package. The operators most similar to you are precisely the ones you were never going to hear about. Absence of news is not evidence of absence.
  >
  > **2. "We already got a no."**
  > One conversation went badly — a banker, an authority staffer, an advisor — and it settled the question. But a no is one sample, from one seat, answering one lens. And notice: the operators who got a different answer on the second or third conversation did not publish that either. You heard your own no. You did not hear anyone else's yes. That's not a market signal; that's a sample size of one with a reporting bias attached.
  >
  > **3. "The process is a black box."**
  > This is the one I'd most like to retire, because it's backwards. Completed conduit deals publish their own paperwork — official statements are public documents, filed and searchable, describing the borrower, the project, the security, the covenants, and the continuing obligations in detail no private financing would ever show you. The record is unusually open. What's missing is that nobody hands it to you and says "start here." The opacity isn't in the documents; it's in the fact that no one tells you they exist.
  >
  > Which suggests a cheap first move that has nothing to do with hiring anyone. Find three completed deals that look like yours — same sector, roughly your size — and read their official statements. Not to copy them. To find out what a lender was actually shown, in what order, and what the borrower had to be able to say about itself.
  >
  > Ninety minutes with three public documents will retire more of your assumptions than any advisor conversation, and it costs nothing. The information asymmetry in this market is smaller than it feels. It just isn't handed to you.
  >
  > *(We run the operator-side readiness and disclosure system on our own live engagements. Everything above is generic to conduit finance and reflects no client's file.)*

**Honest-positioning check:** no client numbers, no invented figures, no named private deals. The public-filing point is a verifiable fact about municipal disclosure, not a claim about our track record.
