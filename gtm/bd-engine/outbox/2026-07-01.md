## Content (1 post) — Angle 1: Deal Anatomy
**Topic:** Walk a publicly-filed conduit deal stage by stage — where the operator actually spent time. *(Longest-unused usable angle, last ran 06-24. Honest positioning; our-system framing; no client numbers. Angle 4/proof-story still blocked until Action B lands.)*

> **Anatomy of a conduit bond deal — the four stages, and the one that actually hurts.**
>
> People think a tax-exempt facility bond is a financing event. It's four stages, and they're wildly uneven:
>
> **1. Eligibility** — does the project type qualify (manufacturing, solid-waste, affordable housing, healthcare)? Usually a fast yes/no. Days.
> **2. Readiness story** — can you tell the facts a lender trusts, in the order they expect them? Weeks-to-months. This is where "you're not ready" lives — and it's almost never about the money.
> **3. Disclosure package** — the paper that survives scrutiny. Tedious, unglamorous, and the single biggest silent time-sink. Every missing document is a round-trip.
> **4. Issuer + market** — board approval, pricing, close. Feels like the finish line; it's Stage 4 of 4.
>
> Operators brace for Stage 1 (the "am I even allowed" fear) and Stage 4 (the "will it price" fear). The one that actually eats the calendar is **Stage 3** — and it's the most fixable. We built the system we use to keep that package deal-ready, because we got tired of watching Stage 3 slide a close a full quarter.
>
> If you've got a facility project and you're stuck deciding whether you're "ready," you're probably standing in Stage 2 looking at Stage 3. Name the stage you're in — happy to tell you what actually clears it.
