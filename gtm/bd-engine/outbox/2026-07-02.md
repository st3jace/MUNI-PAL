**Longest-unused usable angle (last ran 06-25). Angle 4/proof-story still blocked by empty proof library.**

> **The 5 questions a conduit lender is actually asking — score yourself 0–5.**
>
> Operators think a bond gets approved on the strength of the project. It gets approved on the strength of the *answers*. Here are five questions every credit committee runs, whether or not they say them out loud:
>
> 1. **Can you name your revenue and prove it repeats?** Not "we'll have customers" — signed offtake, contracts, or a track record. (0 = projections only, 5 = contracted and documented.)
> 2. **Is your disclosure package assembled, or theoretical?** The official statement is 80% paperwork you either have or you don't. (0 = "we'll pull it together," 5 = sitting in a data room.)
> 3. **Do your covenants match how you actually operate?** Covenants written to look good and then breached in month three sink the next deal. (0 = haven't modeled them, 5 = stress-tested against your own cash flow.)
> 4. **Have you sequenced backward from the board date?** Conduit boards meet on a cycle — miss a packet deadline and the close slides a full quarter. (0 = no calendar, 5 = deadlines mapped.)
> 5. **Is there someone on your side who's closed one before?** (0 = first rodeo alone, 5 = an operator who's run it.)
>
> Add it up. Under 15 and you're not "not bond-ready" — you're bond-ready with a gap list, which is a very different, very fixable thing. Happy to send the long-form version of this check to anyone who wants it.

*Positioning is honest: it's a framework we run, not a claimed client outcome. No fabricated numbers per hard rule 3.*
