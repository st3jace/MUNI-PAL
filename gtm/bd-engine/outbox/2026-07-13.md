Fresh cut distinct from the prior angle-3 takes — 06-22 document-churn, 06-29 board-calendar-slip, 07-06 premature-no: this one prices the cost of delay in the operator's own P&L (carry / lease / idle throughput per stalled quarter), not process friction. Honest positioning, no client numbers, the only "we" claim is that we run the system live.*

> **The most expensive line item in a bond deal isn't on the term sheet. It's the quarters you spend not-financed while you "get ready."**
>
> Operators price the fee. They rarely price the clock.
>
> A facility project that sits unfinanced for two extra quarters is still paying for itself the whole time — the lease on the site, the equipment deposits, the crew you're holding, the throughput the plant isn't producing yet. That's real money leaving every month, and none of it shows up as a "cost of the bond." It shows up as carry.
>
> Here's the part that stings: most of that delay isn't the market and isn't the issuer. It's readiness. The disclosure package gets assembled reactively, the info requests come back in waves, and the review clock resets each time — so a deal that could have closed in one board cycle takes three.
>
> The fix isn't cheaper counsel. It's sequencing: build the readiness story and the disclosure package *before* you're in front of the issuer, so the process runs forward instead of looping. The fee is fixed. The carry is the variable you actually control.
>
> *(We run the readiness system on live conduit engagements — the first conversation is free, and you leave knowing which quarter you're about to lose and how to keep it.)*
