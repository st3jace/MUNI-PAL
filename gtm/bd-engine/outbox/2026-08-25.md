- [ ] **Post draft:**

  > Every readiness checklist you will be handed looks like bureaucracy. Twenty or thirty items, no order, no explanation, apparently invented by someone who enjoys paperwork.
  >
  > They are not twenty or thirty items. **They are three questions, asked over and over in different costumes.**
  >
  > Here they are:
  >
  > **1. Will the thing get built?**
  > **2. Will it produce the money?**
  > **3. Who is answerable if it doesn't?**
  >
  > That is the entire underwrite. Every item on the list is evidence for one of those three, and nothing else. Site control, permits, the construction budget, the contractor — question 1. Operating history, the revenue model, the feasibility work, the market — question 2. Entity structure, governance, financials, who signs, who is on the hook — question 3.
  >
  > Why this matters more than it sounds:
  >
  > **An operator who knows which question an item answers produces a usable version on the first try. An operator who doesn't produces something technically responsive and useless.** Asked for board minutes, the first operator sends the resolution that shows who was authorized to sign, for how much, and for how long — because they understand it is question 3. The second sends the whole year of minutes, because it was on a list.
  >
  > That difference is not intelligence or effort. It is knowing what is being asked.
  >
  > Two more things fall out of it:
  >
  > **A gap in one question is not the same as a gap in another.** Being thin on question 1 usually means waiting — permits and site control run on other people's clocks. Being thin on question 3 usually means an afternoon of paperwork. Operators routinely conclude they are "years away" on the strength of a question-3 gap, which is the cheapest kind there is.
  >
  > **An item that answers nothing should be challenged.** If you cannot place a request under one of the three, ask. Sometimes there is a good reason you cannot see from your seat. Sometimes it is a form that got copied forward for a decade. Both answers are useful, and asking reads as competence rather than resistance.
  >
  > **The give-away, free, use it today:** take whatever readiness list you have been handed. Put a 1, 2 or 3 beside every line. Then count.
  >
  > If one number dominates, that is where your actual gap is, and it tells you whether you are waiting on the world or waiting on yourself. If a line gets no number, that is your next email.
  >
  > Most operators have never done this and are surprised by the count. The list stops being arbitrary the moment it is sorted by what it is for.
  >
  > *(We run the operator-side readiness and disclosure system on our own live engagements. The above is generic to conduit finance; diligence lists, issuer processes and counsel practice vary considerably by state and by issuer, and reflect no client's file. Confirm your own situation with bond counsel and a municipal advisor before acting on any of it.)*
