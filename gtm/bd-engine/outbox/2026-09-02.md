- [ ] **Post draft:**

  > A readiness checklist looks like a to-do list. Items, done or not done, and the goal is to get everything into the "done" column as early as you can.
  >
  > That instinct is right about roughly half the list and quietly expensive on the other half.
  >
  > **Some items on a readiness list are permanent once produced.** Your entity documents. Your ownership chart. The history of what this organisation has actually done. Producing these early is pure gain — they do not get worse while they sit, and everything downstream is easier once they exist.
  >
  > **Other items begin decaying the moment they are produced.** A statement of position describes a date that keeps receding. A valuation describes a market that keeps moving. An authorisation to sign carries a scope and a period. A letter of intent has an outside date. A permit and an insurance certificate have expiry printed on them. A third-party report describes conditions that were true when the work was done.
  >
  > None of these are wrong when they go stale. They are just **no longer something a stranger can rely on** — and being relied on by a stranger is the entire job of a diligence item.
  >
  > **So the checklist has a second column nobody draws: not "is it done," but "how long does done last."**
  >
  > This is where the expensive version of preparation happens. An operator told to "get ready" does the responsible thing and produces everything they can as fast as they can. Months later the financing calendar arrives and a handful of those items — the ones that decay — have to be produced **again**, at full cost, because they aged out while the rest of the deal caught up. The work was real. It was just spent early on the wrong subset.
  >
  > **The fix is not more work; it is sequencing, and it is free.** Three questions, asked once per item, of whoever will actually rely on it:
  >
  > **Does this item have a window at all?** Many do not. Those are your early wins — do them now, in the quiet, when nobody is waiting on you.
  > **If it does, how long is the window, and who sets it?** The answer almost never comes from you. It comes from the party who will rely on it — an issuer's policy, counsel's requirement, a lender's standard, a statute, or the document's own expiry date. **Do not guess this and do not carry someone else's answer over from another deal, sector or year.**
  > **When does the clock start?** As-of date, issue date, signature date and delivery date are four different things, and which one starts the clock decides whether an item is comfortably fresh or three weeks from useless.
  >
  > **Then reorder.** Permanent items first, immediately, regardless of whether a project exists yet. Decaying items **last**, deliberately scheduled backwards from the date somebody will read them — and the long-lead ones flagged early, because "must be recent" and "takes eight weeks to obtain" together is the squeeze that actually moves closing dates.
  >
  > **The exercise, and it costs nothing.** Take the readiness list you already have. Put a **P** beside every item that is permanent once produced and a **D** beside every item that decays. You will not be certain about all of them — the uncertain ones are the list. For each **D** and each unsure item, one message to the party who will rely on it: *how recent does this need to be at closing, and what date does that run from?*
  >
  > What comes back is a schedule, not a to-do list. It is the same set of items, and it costs far less to produce in the right order.
  >
  > *(We run the operator-side readiness and disclosure system on our own live engagements. The above is generic to conduit finance and reflects no client's file. Which items decay, how long their windows run, and what starts the clock vary by sector, structure, issuer, lender and jurisdiction — this post deliberately states no periods, because the only reliable source for them is the party relying on the item. Nothing here is legal, tax or financial advice — confirm your own situation with bond counsel and a municipal advisor.)*
