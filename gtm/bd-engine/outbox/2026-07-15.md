*Longest-unused usable angle (last ran 07-08; recent window cycled 3→2→1→5→6→3→2). FRESH cut distinct from 07-01's stage-list walkthrough and 07-08's time-distribution take: the single moment in a conduit deal that surprises operators most — when informal interest has to become documented fact. Honest positioning, anonymized/composite, no client numbers. Angle 4/proof-story still blocked by empty proof library.*

- [ ] **Post draft:**

> **The moment every first-time conduit borrower gets surprised — and it isn't the interest rate.**
>
> Walk a conduit/IRB deal from the operator's chair and there's one predictable point where the mood in the room changes. It's not the rate conversation. It's not the board vote. It's the moment the deal crosses from *"everyone's interested"* to *"now prove it on paper."*
>
> Up to that point, momentum feels great. The issuer is encouraging. Counsel is friendly. The project makes obvious sense to anyone who hears it. Then the diligence list arrives, and the operator realizes the deal was never going to be decided on how good the project *sounds* — it's decided on whether the *documented record* holds up: the financials tie out, the project story is the same in every exhibit, the eligibility basis is airtight, the covenants are ones you can actually live with.
>
> That gap — between a project that's genuinely fundable and a *file* that survives scrutiny — is where first deals stall. Not because the operator wasn't ready to build. Because nobody told them the real test was the paper, and they started assembling it the week it was due instead of the quarter before.
>
> The anatomy underneath: a fundable deal needs (1) a credible readiness story lenders trust, (2) a complete disclosure package that survives scrutiny, and (3) someone who's walked the process before and knows what the list will ask *before* it arrives. Most operators show up with a great project and one of those three.
>
> If you've got a facility project and you can feel the "everyone's interested" phase happening right now — the useful move is to start building the paper this quarter, not the week the diligence list lands. Happy to show anyone what that list actually contains, free.
