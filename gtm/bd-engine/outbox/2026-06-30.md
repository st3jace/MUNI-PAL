## Content (1 post) — *angle 2: Readiness myth-busting (longest-unused usable angle; last ran 06-23 — three fresh myths this time)*

- [ ] **Post — "Three things people think you need to issue a bond. You don't."**

> Operators talk themselves out of conduit/tax-exempt financing over requirements that aren't real. Three of the most common:

> **Myth 1: "You need an investment-grade credit rating."** No. A large share of conduit and industrial-revenue deals are non-rated or privately placed with a single institutional buyer. The rating is a *distribution* choice — how widely you sell the paper — not an eligibility gate. Plenty of fundable projects never get rated at all.

> **Myth 2: "You need collateral the way a bank wants collateral."** A conduit revenue bond is typically secured by the *project's* revenues and a defined security package — not pledged against your corporate or personal balance sheet the way a commercial loan is. Operators who assume "I don't have enough to pledge" are often measuring against the wrong structure.

> **Myth 3: "You apply, and the issuer decides whether they like your project."** A conduit issuer is largely a pass-through. Eligibility turns on the project *category* and public benefit, not a beauty contest. The decision that actually matters is the buyer's — and that turns on your readiness package, not the issuer's taste.

> The pattern under all three: the real gate is almost never the one operators fear. It's whether the readiness story and disclosure are assembled well enough for a buyer to say yes. That part is knowable and buildable.
