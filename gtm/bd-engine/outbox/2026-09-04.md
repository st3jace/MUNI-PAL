- [ ] **Post draft:**

  > Every financing has an account of itself. What the project is, why now, where the money goes, what changed since the last version of the plan, what that odd item on the balance sheet is, why the entity is structured the way it is.
  >
  > On a lot of operator-side deals, **that account has never been written down.** It lives in one person's head — usually the founder, the executive director, or the controller who has been there longest — and it is produced fresh, out loud, every time a new party joins.
  >
  > That feels free. It is one of the more expensive things in the deal.
  >
  > **Here is where the cost lands.**
  >
  > **It drifts, and the drift becomes the question.** Told from memory, the same true fact comes out slightly differently each time — a different starting date, a different reason emphasised, a rounder number. Nobody is lying. But a diligence reader is comparing what you said against what your documents show, and each difference has to be run to ground before anyone can move on. **You end up defending the variance rather than the facts.** The facts were never the problem.
  >
  > **It makes one person a hard dependency.** If the account exists only in someone's head, every question routes to that person, and only that person can answer it. That is a bottleneck in a normal week. In the week they are on a plane, in surgery, or inside a month-end close, it is a stopped deal — and nobody else in the building can move it, because nobody else has the story.
  >
  > **It is re-produced at senior cost, every single time.** The retelling is not delegable. It is done by the most expensive and least substitutable person you have, at whatever moment the request lands, and it leaves nothing behind — the next party still gets it from scratch.
  >
  > **And it quietly sets the tone.** A team that can hand over a clear written account of its own deal reads as prepared. A team that cannot reads as disorganised, even when every underlying fact is solid and every document is in order. That impression forms early and it is expensive to reverse.
  >
  > **The fix is a document, and the first version can be written this week.**
  >
  > Write your deal's own account down, once. Plain language. No formatting effort, no legal drafting. What the project is. Why it is happening now. What the money does. What changed from any earlier plan, and why. Anything in the numbers or the structure that a stranger would stop and ask about — with the answer sitting next to it. What is still open, and who owns each open item.
  >
  > Then three things turn it from notes into an asset:
  >
  > **Date it and version it.** An undated account rots quietly, and nobody can tell which telling is current.
  >
  > **Make it the thing you send, not the thing you say.** Every new party gets the document first. Their questions then start from a shared baseline instead of from zero — and, importantly, **they correct the document rather than receiving a new telling.** The document gets better with every party. A retelling never does.
  >
  > **Name a second person who can answer from it.** If only the author can use it, you have written the bottleneck down rather than removed it.
  >
  > **What this is not.** It is not a disclosure document, it is not an offering document, and nothing in it reaches investors or a public filing without your bond counsel and municipal advisor deciding what belongs there and how it has to be said. This is an internal working document — the thing you hand your own advisors so they are not reconstructing your business from conversation.
  >
  > That is the point. **Your counsel and your municipal advisor should be spending their hours on your structure, not on assembling a picture of your business that only you can supply.** Every hour they spend interviewing you is an hour you are paying for information you already had.
  >
  > It is one of the few genuinely free improvements available to an operator, and it is very often the one nobody has been assigned.
  >
  > *(We run the operator-side readiness and disclosure system on our own live engagements. The above is generic to conduit finance and reflects no client's file. Nothing here is legal, tax or financial advice; what belongs in any document that reaches a third party is a question for bond counsel and a municipal advisor.)*
