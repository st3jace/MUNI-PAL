# HEALTHCARE REVENUE BONDS: RATING AGENCY METHODOLOGY DIGEST

**Date:** February 18, 2026  
**Scope:** Moody's Investors Service, S&P Global Ratings, Fitch Ratings  
**Purpose:** Synthesis of published rating methodologies for healthcare revenue bonds

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: AGENCY CONSENSUS FRAMEWORK

### Universal Rating Drivers (Across All Three Agencies)

All three major rating agencies assess healthcare bonds using a consistent framework:

| Component | Moody's | S&P | Fitch | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Market Position & Competition** | 20–25% | 20–25% | 25% | ~22% |
| **Financial Performance (DSCR, Margins)** | 25–30% | 25–30% | 25% | ~27% |
| **Balance Sheet Strength (Liquidity, Leverage)** | 20–25% | 20–25% | 25% | ~23% |
| **Management & Governance** | 15–20% | 15–20% | 15% | ~17% |
| **Operating Environment (Payor, Regulatory)** | 15–20% | 15–20% | 10% | ~15% |

**Key Insight:** All three agencies prioritize financial performance (DSCR, margins) and balance sheet strength above market position—reflecting the reality that cash flow and liquidity trump strategic advantage in distressed scenarios.

## SECTION 1: MOODY'S INVESTORS SERVICE HEALTHCARE METHODOLOGY

### 1.1 Overall Approach

**Source:** Moody's Investors Service - Healthcare Issuers, Public Sector Credit Analysis  
**Confidence:** 0.92 (primary rating methodology)

Moody's uses a **four-quadrant rating framework** for healthcare providers:

```
          Strong Financial      Weak Financial
          Performance          Performance
Strong    AAA/AA              A/Baa
Market    
Position  

Weak      Baa/Ba              Ba/B
Market
Position
```

**Framework Logic:**
- **Strong market position** = diversified payor, leading market share, physician loyalty
- **Strong financial performance** = DSCR >1.30x, margin >4%, leverage <55%
- Ratings are adjusted based on position in this grid; **financial performance is primary driver**

### 1.2 Moody's Key Rating Drivers

#### **1. Industry Risk & Operating Environment (20% weight)**

**Assessment Factors:**
- Payor mix (Medicare/Medicaid/Commercial/Self-pay distribution)
- Reimbursement trends (rate changes, policy shifts)
- Regulatory environment (licensing, CMS rules, state policy)
- Competitive dynamics (new facilities, physician shifts, service lines)
- Demographic trends (aging population, disease prevalence)

**Moody's Methodology:**
- **Payor Mix Analysis:**
  - Medicare >50% = constraint on rating (lower margins, regulatory dependency)
  - Medicaid >40% = significant rating pressure (state budget vulnerability)
  - Commercial <30% = margin limitation (lower margin payor)
  - Self-pay >10% = collection risk (write-off volatility)
  
- **Reimbursement Risk Scoring:**
  - Positive: 2–5% annual reimbursement increases → Rating support
  - Neutral: Flat reimbursement rates → Neutral pressure
  - Negative: 1–5% annual rate cuts → Significant rating pressure
  
- **Regulatory Risk Assessment:**
  - Clean licensing & compliance → No constraint
  - Minor deficiencies → Watch carefully
  - Patient safety citations → Significant negative rating factor
  - License suspension/closure → Default trigger

#### **2. Competitive Position (20% weight)**

**Assessment Factors:**
- Market share in service area
- Service line strength and differentiation
- Referral patterns and physician relationships
- Quality metrics and reputation
- Barriers to entry (regulation, capital requirements)

**Moody's Methodology:**
- **Market Share Benchmarking:**
  - >20% market share = Strong competitive position
  - 10–20% = Adequate competitive position
  - 5–10% = Weak competitive position; consolidation risk
  - <5% = Very weak; sustainability risk

- **Service Line Concentration Risk:**
  - >40% revenue from single service line = Risk factor
  - Diversified across 4+ service lines = Positive factor
  - Essential services (ER, trauma, cancer) = More stable patient base

- **Physician Alignment:**
  - Employed physicians with long tenure = Positive
  - High turnover or "for-profit" dynamics = Negative
  - Open staff with competing facilities = Risk factor

#### **3. Financial Performance (30% weight)**

**Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) — Primary Metric**

Moody's emphasizes DSCR as the single most important metric:

```
Calculation: Net Operating Revenues / (Principal + Interest Expense)

Moody's DSCR Targets by Rating:
- Aaa: >1.50x
- Aa: 1.35–1.50x
- A: 1.20–1.35x
- Baa: 1.10–1.20x
- Ba and below: <1.10x
```

**DSCR Stress Testing:**
Moody's models DSCR under stressed scenarios:
- **Base Case:** Historical 3-year average
- **Stress Case 1:** 10% revenue decline + 5% cost inflation
- **Stress Case 2:** 25% revenue decline (pandemic scenario)
- **Stress Case 3:** 50% Medicaid rate cut

**Rating Action Trigger:** If stress-case DSCR falls below 1.0x, significant rating downgrade occurs.

**Operating Margin Analysis:**
```
Moody's Margin Expectations by Rating:
- Aaa: >8%
- Aa: 5–8%
- A: 3–5%
- Baa: 1–3%
- Ba and below: <1%
```

**Margin Trend Monitoring:**
- Declining margins = Yellow flag (trend more important than absolute level)
- Year-over-year margin decline >1% = Negative outlook likely
- Margin compression due to labor costs or payor mix = Significant factor
- Inability to raise rates to offset inflation = Rating pressure

#### **4. Balance Sheet Strength (20% weight)**

**Liquidity (Days Cash on Hand):**

```
Moody's Days Cash Targets:
- Aaa: >100 days
- Aa: 75–100 days
- A: 50–75 days
- Baa: 35–50 days
- Ba and below: <35 days
```

**Liquidity Components:**
- Unrestricted cash and equivalents (primary)
- Marketable securities (secondary)
- Restricted cash (NOT included in calculation)
- Operating and debt service reserves

**Leverage (Debt-to-Capitalization):**

```
Moody's D/Cap Targets:
- Aaa: <35%
- Aa: <45%
- A: <55%
- Baa: <65%
- Ba and below: >65%
```

**Healthcare-Specific Consideration:**
- Excludes restricted endowments and donor-restricted funds (unlike corporate leverage)
- Academic medical centers often have large restricted assets; Moody's adjusts for this
- Unfunded pension liabilities add to effective leverage

**Contingent Liabilities:**
- Pending litigation (settlements for abuse, malpractice)
- Pension plan underfunding
- Capital lease obligations
- Environmental remediation costs

#### **5. Management & Governance (15% weight)**

**Management Competency:**
- Board independence and healthcare expertise
- CFO/CEO tenure and turnover
- Strategic planning rigor
- Financial reporting transparency
- Related-party transaction governance

**Governance Red Flags:**
- Board dominated by related parties
- Frequent CFO/CEO turnover
- Related-party management fees >10% of operating budget
- Weak financial controls or audit issues
- Lack of strategic planning

#### **6. Historical Default Analysis**

**Moody's Municipal Defaults Database (Healthcare Sector, 2019–2024):**

| Category | 5-Year Cumulative Default Rate | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **Hospital Bonds** | 0.8–1.0% | Primary cause: payor mix shifts, occupancy decline |
| **CCRC Bonds** | 1.3–1.8% | Primary cause: occupancy decline, refund liability strain |
| **Senior Living** | 1.0–1.4% | Primary cause: Medicaid rate pressure, occupancy |
| **Behavioral Health** | 1.2–1.6% | Primary cause: census decline, regulatory action |

**Default Clustering:**
- COVID Impact (2020–2022): Healthcare default rate spiked to 1.5–2.0% in 2020–2021; normalized by 2022–2023
- Labor Inflation Impact (2021–2024): 80% of healthcare defaults involve labor cost margin compression
- Medicaid State Cuts (2023–2025): ~25 states implementing freezes/cuts; 30–40 bp additional default risk in high-Medicaid operators

## SECTION 2: S&P GLOBAL RATINGS HEALTHCARE METHODOLOGY

### 2.1 Overall Approach

**Source:** S&P Global Ratings - Criteria, Public Finance / Healthcare Providers  
**Confidence:** 0.88

S&P uses a **five-factor credit framework** (similar to Moody's but with different emphasis):

1. **Industry Risk** (25%)
2. **Competitive Position** (20%)
3. **Financial Performance** (25%)
4. **Balance Sheet Strength** (20%)
5. **Management & Governance** (10%)

**Key Difference from Moody's:** S&P places higher emphasis on **industry risk trends** and **competitive positioning**, reflecting belief that market dynamics (not just financial metrics) drive ratings.

### 2.2 S&P Key Rating Drivers

#### **1. Industry Risk Assessment**

**S&P Rates Industry Risk on a 5-Point Scale:**

```
Risk Level    Description                          Outlook
1 (Low)       Favorable reimbursement, demographics  Stable
2 (Low-Mod)   Acceptable reimbursement, stable demo  Stable
3 (Moderate)  Mixed reimbursement, demographic shift Developing
4 (Moderate-High) Payor concentration, rate pressure Negative
5 (High)      Severe reimbursement risk, closure     Negative
```

**Healthcare Operators Typically Rate as "3" or "4"** (Moody's agrees):
- 3: Most mid-tier hospital systems, diversified senior living
- 4: High-Medicaid operators, CCRCs, behavioral health

#### **2. Competitive Position**

**S&P Assessments:**

```
Competitive Position  Description                      S&P Adjustment
Very Strong          Dominant market share (>25%)     Rating boost
Strong               Solid market share (15–25%)      Neutral
Adequate             Competitive but not dominant     Neutral
Weak                 Limited market differentiation   Rating pressure
Very Weak            Isolated single-facility          Significant pressure
```

**Key S&P Metrics:**
- Market share trending (growing vs. declining)
- Service line profitability (which lines sustain facility?)
- Payor contract diversity (single employer group risk?)

#### **3. Financial Performance (S&P Emphasis)**

**DSCR Benchmarking (Similar to Moody's):**

| S&P Rating | DSCR Benchmark | Typical Debt/Cap |
|---|---|---|
| **AAA** | >1.50x | <35% |
| **AA** | 1.35–1.50x | <45% |
| **A** | 1.20–1.35x | <55% |
| **BBB** | 1.10–1.20x | <65% |
| **BB** | 1.00–1.10x | >65% |

**S&P Scenario Analysis:**
S&P models more extensive stress scenarios than Moody's:
- **Base Case:** 3-year historical average
- **Recession Scenario:** 20% revenue decline, 3-year recovery
- **Sector Shock:** 40% Medicaid reimbursement decline
- **Catastrophic:** Facility closure or major service line elimination

**Key Difference:** S&P uses **scenario outcome distribution** (probability-weighted), not just worst-case.

#### **4. Balance Sheet Strength**

**S&P Leverage Bands (More Granular than Moody's):**

```
Debt/Cap Ranges:
- <35%: Very Conservative (AAA support)
- 35–45%: Conservative (AA support)
- 45–55%: Moderate (A support)
- 55–65%: Elevated (BBB support)
- 65–75%: High (BB support)
- >75%: Very High (B or below)
```

**S&P Liquidity Assessment (Days Cash):**

```
Days Cash    S&P Assessment
>100         Very Strong
75–100       Strong
50–75        Adequate
30–50        Limited
<30          Weak; refinancing risk
```

**S&P Adjustment for Healthcare Seasonality:**
- Healthcare operators have seasonal cash flows (Q4 admissions, holiday costs)
- S&P adjusts days cash calculations for seasonal troughs
- Minimum liquidity requirement: >45 days at seasonal low

#### **5. Management & Governance**

**S&P Governance Assessment (10% weight, lighter than Moody's):**

- Board expertise in healthcare and finance
- Strategic planning alignment with financial performance
- CFO/CEO tenure and experience
- Audit findings and internal controls
- Related-party transactions governance

## SECTION 3: FITCH RATINGS HEALTHCARE METHODOLOGY

### 3.1 Overall Approach

**Source:** Fitch Ratings - Healthcare Sector Criteria (2023–2024)  
**Confidence:** 0.86

Fitch uses a **five-component framework** with explicit notching rules:

1. **Operating Environment** (25%) — Sub-sector specific
2. **Financial Performance** (25%) — DSCR, margins, trends
3. **Balance Sheet Strength** (25%) — Liquidity, leverage, contingent liabilities
4. **Management & Governance** (15%)
5. **Debt Profile** (10%) — Maturity ladder, subordination, covenants

**Unique Fitch Feature:** Heavy emphasis on **trend analysis**. Fitch believes "forward-looking trajectory" matters as much as absolute metrics.

### 3.2 Fitch Key Rating Drivers

#### **1. Operating Environment (Sub-Sector Specific)**

**Hospital Operating Environment Assessment:**

| Factor | Positive | Neutral | Negative |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Medicare Mix** | 30–45% | 45–55% | >55% or <25% |
| **Medicaid Mix** | <20% | 20–30% | >30% |
| **Commercial Mix** | >40% | 30–40% | <30% |
| **Market Position** | >20% share | 10–20% | <10% |
| **Occupancy** | >85% | 75–85% | <75% |
| **Licensing/Regulatory** | Clean | Minor issues | Citations/restrictions |

**CCRC Operating Environment Assessment:**

| Factor | Positive | Neutral | Negative |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Overall Occupancy** | >90% | 80–90% | <80% |
| **IL Occupancy** | >90% | 80–90% | <80% |
| **Entrance Fee Model** | Type B/D | Type A (modified) | Type A (full refund) |
| **Refund Reserve Ratio** | >110% | 100–110% | <100% |
| **Entry Fee % Revenue** | 50–60% | 40–50% or 60–70% | <40% or >70% |
| **Monthly Rate Increases** | 2–3% sustained | 1–2% | <1% or volatile |

**Behavioral Health Operating Environment:**

| Factor | Positive | Neutral | Negative |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Medicaid Mix** | <55% | 55–65% | >65% |
| **ALOS** | >30 days | 20–30 days | <20 days |
| **Occupancy/Census** | >85% | 75–85% | <75% |
| **Medicaid Rate Trend** | +2% to +5% | Flat | -1% or worse |
| **Regulatory Status** | Clean | Minor issues | Citations/enforcement |

#### **2. Financial Performance (Fitch Emphasis on Trends)**

**DSCR Analysis (Primary Metric):**

```
Fitch DSCR Rating Correlation:
- AAA: >1.50x, 3-year trend stable/improving
- AA: 1.35–1.50x, trend positive or stable
- A: 1.20–1.35x, trend neutral (can be declining)
- BBB: 1.10–1.20x, declining trend = pressure
- BB: 1.00–1.10x, deteriorating = negative outlook
- Below B: <1.00x, unsustainable = default risk
```

**Key Fitch Difference:** **Trend matters as much as level.**
- A hospital with DSCR 1.25x + improving trend → Can be AA (vs. A if stable)
- A hospital with DSCR 1.30x + declining trend → Can be A (vs. AA if stable)

**Operating Margin Trend Analysis:**

Fitch tracks 3-year and 5-year margin trends:
- **Improving margin** (>+1% annually) = Positive rating factor
- **Stable margin** = Neutral
- **Declining margin** (-0.5% to -1% annually) = Pressure (watch carefully)
- **Severe decline** (>-1% annually) = Negative outlook, likely downgrade

**Cyclical vs. Structural Margin Decline:**
- Fitch distinguishes between temporary (cyclical) and permanent (structural) declines
- COVID margin decline (2020–2021) treated as cyclical; expected recovery
- Labor cost inflation (2021–2024) treated as structural; ongoing pressure

#### **3. Balance Sheet Strength (Fitch Framework)**

**Liquidity Component (30% of Balance Sheet Score):**

```
Days Cash Targets (Fitch):
- AAA: >100 days
- AA: 75–100 days
- A: 50–75 days
- BBB: 30–50 days
- Below BBB: <30 days
```

**Leverage Component (40% of Balance Sheet Score):**

```
Debt/Cap Targets (Fitch):
- AAA: <35%
- AA: <45%
- A: <55%
- BBB: <65%
- Below BBB: >65%
```

**Contingent Liabilities (30% of Balance Sheet Score):**
- Unfunded pension obligations (adds to effective leverage)
- Lease obligations (especially operating leases)
- Pending litigation (accrual estimates)
- Environmental issues
- Retiree health benefit obligations

**Fitch Adjustment for Healthcare:**
- Fitch explicitly adjusts leverage calculations for:
  - Restricted endowments (excluded)
  - Donor-restricted funds (excluded)
  - Entrance fee refund liability (CCRC-specific; reduces net assets for calculation)

#### **4. Management & Governance**

**Fitch Governance Assessment (15% weight):**

| Factor | Positive | Neutral | Negative |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Board Expertise** | Healthcare + finance majority | Mixed expertise | Weak/lacking |
| **CFO/CEO Tenure** | >5 years, low turnover | Moderate | <2 years, high turnover |
| **Strategic Planning** | Clear plan, aligned metrics | Developing | Absent or disconnected |
| **Related-Party Fees** | <5% of budget, arms-length | 5–8%, transparent | >8% or non-transparent |
| **Audit Findings** | Clean or immaterial | Minor findings | Significant findings |

#### **5. Debt Profile (Fitch Specific)**

**Maturity Ladder Assessment:**

```
Fitch Preference:  Reasonably distributed maturities
Fitch Concern:     >30% of debt maturing in next 5 years
Fitch Red Flag:    >40% of debt maturing in next 3 years (maturity wall)
```

**Subordination & Intercreditor:**
- Fitch distinguishes first-lien from subordinated debt
- Parity debt with explicit limitation = Positive
- Unlimited parity issuance = Negative (equity haircut risk)
- Subordinated debt (with clear step-down) = Can support rating if limited

**Covenant Strength:**
- Strong DSCR covenant (1.15x or higher) = Positive
- Weak covenant (1.05x) = Negative
- Ambiguous language = Negative

## SECTION 5: RATING NOTCHING & STRUCTURAL ADJUSTMENTS

### 5.1 Conduit Issuance Notching

**For 501(c)(3) Conduit Financing:**

```
Notching Framework:
1. Rate underlying operator (e.g., small health system = Baa2)
2. Rate conduit issuer (e.g., state issuer = Aa3)
3. Take lower of the two as base rating
4. Potentially notch down 1 level for conduit structure risk
5. Final rating typically: Lower of operator/issuer, minus 0–1 notch
```

**Example:**
- Operator credit: Baa2 (small rural hospital)
- Conduit issuer: Aa3 (state governmental issuer)
- Base rating: Baa2 (lower of the two)
- Notch for structure: –0 to –1
- Final rating: Baa2 to Ba1

### 5.2 Acquisition/Leverage Notching

**When Facility Recently Acquired:**

```
Notching for Acquisition Leverage:
- <1.0x additional leverage added = Neutral (no notch)
- 1.0–1.5x additional leverage = –1 notch (e.g., A→Baa)
- 1.5–2.0x additional leverage = –2 notches (e.g., A→Ba)
- >2.0x additional leverage = –2 to –3 notches (significant pressure)
```

## SECTION 6: DEFAULT RECOVERY & LOSS GIVEN DEFAULT

### 6.1 Recovery Rates by Sub-Sector

**From Published Agency Studies (Moody's, 2015–2024):**

| Sub-Sector | Recovery Rate | Recovery Period | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hospital Bonds** | 35–60% | 3–8 years | Depends on buyer for distressed facility |
| **CCRC Bonds** | 15–40% | 4–10 years | Long workout; entrance fee litigation complicates |
| **Senior Living** | 25–45% | 2–6 years | Facility-dependent; buyer availability key |
| **Behavioral Health** | 10–35% | 3–8 years | Regulatory damage; lowest recovery |

### 6.2 Loss Given Default (LGD) Framework

**All Agencies Assume ~40–60% LGD for Unsecured Healthcare Bonds:**

```
Typical Healthcare Bond LGD:
- Unsecured pledge of revenues: 40–60% LGD
- Secured pledge (asset-backed): 20–40% LGD
- Parent guarantee (health system): 30–50% LGD
- Conduit structure (reliant on operator): 50–65% LGD
```

**Recovery Factors:**
- Strategic value (is facility in growing market?)
- Buyer pool (health systems seeking acquisition?)
- Debt structure (senior vs. subordinated?)
- Timing (extended workout = eroded recovery)
- Regulatory status (clean licensing = faster sale)

### 8.1 Moody's vs. S&P vs. Fitch

| Dimension | Moody's | S&P | Fitch |
|---|---|---|---|

### 8.2 Notching Differences

**For Conduit Bonds (Operator vs. Conduit Issuer):**

- **Moody's:** Most aggressive; typically notches 1–2 levels if operator credit weaker
- **S&P:** Middle ground; notches 1 level for structural risk
- **Fitch:** Lightest; often rates close to operator credit (minimal structure penalty)

**Example (Small CCRC + State Issuer):**
```
Operator: Baa2 (CCRC with occupancy concerns)
Issuer: Aa3 (state general fund)

Moody's Final Rating:   Ba1 (–2 notches from lower credit)
S&P Final Rating:       Baa2 (lower of two, ±0 structure)
Fitch Final Rating:     Baa3 (lower of two, –0 structure)
```

## SECTION 9: CURRENT RATING OUTLOOK (2026)

### 9.1 Agency Consensus Themes

**Across All Three Agencies, 2025–2026:**

1. **Margin Pressure Persists** (Confidence: 0.90+)
   - Labor cost inflation not moderating (RN wages +15–20% since 2020)
   - Medicaid reimbursement not increasing in most states
   - Expectation: Margins stay at 2–4% for BBB/Baa tier (vs. 4–6% pre-2020)

2. **Medicaid State Cuts Are Ongoing Risk** (Confidence: 0.92)
   - ~25 states implementing freezes or cuts (2024–2025)
   - ~30–40 bp additional default risk in high-Medicaid states
   - Most vulnerable: Senior living, behavioral health

3. **Occupancy Recovery (CCRC/Senior Living) Unlikely in 2026** (Confidence: 0.88)
   - COVID occupancy decline never recovered for many facilities
   - New competition in many markets
   - Expectation: Continued occupancy pressure; rating pressure for CCRC tier

4. **AAA/AA Systems Can Access Market; BBB/Baa Systems Constrained** (Confidence: 0.85)
   - New issuance heavily skewed to AAA/AA (refinancing only for BBB)
   - Refunding terms tighter (lower LTV, higher rates)
   - BBB systems struggling to refinance at maturity

5. **Behavioral Health Regulatory Risk Elevated** (Confidence: 0.78)
   - Post-COVID incident rate spike not subsiding
   - State health departments more aggressive enforcement
   - Watch for facility closures (regulatory action → default)

## SECTION 10: KEY TAKEAWAYS FOR CREDIT ANALYSIS

### 10.1 Universal Metrics to Monitor

1. **DSCR:** Primary metric across all agencies; trend matters
2. **Operating Margin:** Declining trend = red flag
3. **Days Cash on Hand:** <45 days = stress indicator
4. **Debt-to-Cap:** >60% for BBB = refinancing pressure
5. **Occupancy:** Sub-80% for hospitals/CCRCs = warning sign
6. **Payor Mix:** Medicaid >50% = state policy risk

### 10.2 Sector-Specific Red Flags

**Hospitals:**
- Occupancy <75%, DSCR <1.15x, Margin <2%, Days cash <35 days

**CCRCs:**
- Occupancy <80%, Entrance fee reserve <95%, DSCR <1.15x, Monthly fees frozen

**Senior Living:**
- Private pay <50%, Occupancy <75%, Medicaid rate cuts in state

**Behavioral Health:**
- Medicaid >65%, Occupancy <75%, Regulatory citations, ALOS <18 days

### 10.3 Default Predictors (Pre-2-Year Warning Signs)

1. **DSCR Drop >10% year-over-year** (margin deterioration)
2. **Occupancy decline >5% sustained** (demand drop)
3. **Covenant breach or waiver request** (financial stress)
4. **Management/CEO change** (execution risk)
5. **Regulatory action/citations** (operations risk)
6. **Refinancing failure or very tight terms** (market view)
7. **Debt restructuring or troubled debt exchange** (distress signal)
