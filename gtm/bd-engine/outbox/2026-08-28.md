- [ ] **Post draft:**

  > Most operators who believe they cannot access bond financing are not carrying a lie. They are carrying something **true** — that has been stripped of the two facts which made it true.
  >
  > **A date. And a scope.**
  >
  > Somebody once said "projects like yours don't get done that way." At the time they said it, they may well have been right. Right about that sector, that structure, that issuer's policy, that market, that year. Then the sentence got repeated. And repeating a sentence is very efficient at moving the claim and very bad at moving the conditions attached to it.
  >
  > So it arrives at you as a fact about the world. It began life as a report about a specific situation — and you were never handed the part that would let you check whether the situation is yours.
  >
  > **You can see this happen in ordinary sentences:**
  >
  > *"Our sector doesn't really use conduit financing."* — Said by whom? About which end of the sector? Reporting what they personally saw, or what somebody told *them*?
  >
  > *"That issuer won't look at a project like ours."* — An issuer's appetite is a **policy**. Policies are written by people, reviewed periodically, and revised. A policy you heard about second-hand is a snapshot, not a permanent property of the institution.
  >
  > *"You need to be much further along before anyone will talk to you."* — Further along **by which measure**, and further along than what? This one is almost always true of *something*. Whether it is true of your project, at this stage, with this issuer, is a different sentence entirely.
  >
  > **The point is not that these are wrong.** Plenty of them are correct, and finding out they are correct is genuinely useful — it tells you where the real work is. The point is that **you cannot tell which is which from inside the sentence**, and undated advice is treated with exactly the same confidence as fresh advice.
  >
  > **A free test you can run this afternoon.** Write down every "we can't" you are carrying about financing. For each one, answer three questions:
  >
  > **1. Who told me?** A person who does this work, or a person who once heard it?
  > **2. Roughly when?** If you cannot put a year on it, that is the finding.
  > **3. Was it about a project like mine — or about theirs?** Same sector, same size, same structure, same issuer?
  >
  > Any belief that survives all three is real information and should shape your plan. **Any belief that cannot answer them is not a fact. It is an inheritance** — and it is currently doing the work of a fact in your decision-making.
  >
  > **Then date the ones that matter.** The cheapest way to date a rule is to ask somebody positioned to revise it: the conduit issuer's staff, bond counsel, a municipal advisor. "Is that still your policy, and would it apply to a project like this one?" That is a short, unembarrassing question, and the people who can answer it are generally willing to.
  >
  > The expensive version is to spend two years working around a rule that nobody has checked since before you had a project.
